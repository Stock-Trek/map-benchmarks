| Concurrent read-only | `leapfrog` (`u64`), `papaya` / `crossbeam-skiplist` (`String`) | `leapfrog` wins all u64 sync variants; `papaya` 3-thread String read-only 691.52 µs; `crossbeam-skiplist` 2-thread String read-only 644.20 µs. |
| Persistent/immutable snapshots | `rpds-hash-trie-map` | Constant 4.3 ns clone; ideal only if mutations after cloning are rare or small. |
| Cannot use a custom/shared hasher (default-hasher comparison only) | `rustc-hash` for serial, `dashmap`/`leapfrog`/`txmap` for concurrent | `btreemap`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `immutable-chunkmap`, `intmap`, and `rustc-hash` cannot enter same-hasher groups. |

### 3.5 Selection guidance and trade-offs

1. **Optimize for the dominant operation.** There is no overall champion. A map
   that is best at iteration (`indexmap`), cloning (`rpds`), integer lookup
   (`intmap`), or single-key `u64` contention (`leapfrog`) is not a general-purpose
   winner.
2. **Treat out-of-the-box and same-hasher results as separate worlds.** The
   `out-of-the-box` groups give each map its default hasher and answer "what happens
   if I just use the crate as shipped?" The `same-hasher` groups remove that variable
   and answer "which map design is better when hashing cost is equal?" Do not compare
   a time from one mode with a time from the other as if only the map changed.
3. **Use the close-contender counts, not just wins.** `ahash` (28/64 within 10%),
   `hashbrown` (23/64), and `std` (16/64) are frequently within a few percent of the
   serial winner. If dependency/API constraints favor one of them, the benchmark
   evidence usually does not justify a large rewrite for raw speed alone.
4. **Use specialization deliberately.** `intmap` and `leapfrog` are restricted by
   key type; `rpds` and `imbl` trade write cost for cheap snapshots; `indexmap`
   trades general lookup performance for iteration/order. These are appropriate only
   when the specialized operation dominates the workload.
5. **Choose concurrent maps by key type and contention pattern.** `txmap` is the
   broad default; `leapfrog` is the `u64` high-contention specialist; `dashmap` is
   the `String` 2-thread synchronization specialist; `papaya`/`crossbeam-skiplist`
   are read-only niche winners. `scc` and `starshard` are not competitive defaults
   in this data.
6. **Do not over-read small differences.** In same-hasher lookup/miss and
   key-sensitivity groups, the top four are often within 1–8%. Those differences are
   small enough that API fit, dependency policy, and workload details should decide.

The more important observation is that several **winning** 100K String growth and
3-thread String synchronization rows have MAD in the 6–7% range. Those wins are
consistent in sign but their exact margins should be treated as approximate.

### 4.3 Fast-path noise

Some fast rows have a low MAD but a high CV: `create`/`rustc-hash` has 0.20% MAD but
24.58% CV; `remove_same-hasher_u64`/`horde` has 1.17% MAD but 20.15% CV; the two
1K/10K `clone-then-write` `rustc-hash` winners have 2.88%/1.47% MAD but 19.61%/19.44%
CV; the 3-thread `u64` Zipfian `leapfrog` contention winners have ~0.2% MAD but
15–19% CV. In these cases the typical iteration is stable but occasional slow
iterations inflate the standard deviation. Because this report uses medians, the
headline times are still representative; it would be inappropriate to use the CV
alone to discard these results.

### 4.4 Implications for close calls

Close contenders should be judged against their own noise. For example, the four
same-hasher hit-lookup leaders at `u64` are 361.7–373.3 ns (1.00–1.03×) with MADs of
0.17–1.43%; the ordering is internally consistent but the practical gap is tiny.
Conversely, a 2–5% gap in a row with 6% MAD is not a reliable ranking. The safest
rules are:

- Use the winner when the gap is >10% and the winner's MAD is <5%.
- Treat gaps of 1–5% as ties unless corroborated by another operation or a second
  run.
- Treat 100K `String<32>` growth and 3-thread `String<32>` synchronization margins
  as directionally useful but not precise.
- Do not compare medians across out-of-the-box and same-hasher groups; the hasher
  changes with the group.

### 5.2 Per-implementation wrapper notes

These notes are taken from the modules in `src/maps/`. They describe what the
benchmark wrapper can and cannot exercise; they are not claims about what each crate
supports outside this harness.

- **`ahash`** (`src/maps/ahash_benchmap.rs`): `K: Hash + Eq` and `V: Clone` for
  lookups/inserts; `H: BuildHasher`, with `ahash::RandomState` as the default.
  `new_with_hasher` is supported (so it enters same-hasher groups), and clone needs
  `K: Clone` and `H: Clone`. Insert/remove/get-or-insert use `&mut self`, so the
  wrapper does not enter concurrent groups. Clear is available.
- **`btreemap`** (`src/maps/btreemap_benchmap.rs`): `K: Ord` (not `Hash + Eq`) and
  `V: Clone`. No `new_with_hasher`, so it is absent from same-hasher and
  key-sensitivity. Clone additionally needs `K: Clone`; writes are `&mut`.
- **`crossbeam-skiplist`** (`src/maps/crossbeam_skiplist_benchmap.rs`): ordered keys
  (`K: Ord`); insert/clear require `K: Ord + Send + 'static` and `V: Send +
  'static`; value-returning get/get-or-insert/remove require `V: Clone`, and remove
  additionally requires `K`/`V`: `Send + 'static`. There is no `BenchMapClone`
  implementation, so it cannot enter clone or clone-then-write. No custom hasher is
  supported. Shared-reference writes are available.
- **`dashmap`** (`src/maps/dashmap_benchmap.rs`): `K: Hash + Eq`; `H: BuildHasher +
  Clone` (plus `Default` for `new`); value-returning paths require `V: Clone`.
  `new_with_hasher` and shared-reference get-or-insert are supported. Clone needs
  `K: Clone`. No unusual ergonomic requirement beyond the hasher bounds.
- **`hashbrown`** (`src/maps/hashbrown_benchmap.rs`): `K: Hash + Eq` for
  lookup/insert/remove, `V: Clone` for value-returning paths, `H: BuildHasher`.
  `new_with_hasher` is supported; clone needs `K: Clone`. Writes are `&mut`, so it is
  serial-only. Clear is available.
- **`hashlink`** (`src/maps/hashlink_benchmap.rs`): `H: Default` for `new`;
  `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone` for value-returning paths; clone needs `K: Clone`.
  `new_with_hasher` is supported, but writes are `&mut`, so it is serial-only.
- **`horde`** (`src/maps/horde_benchmap.rs`): `K: Hash + Eq` (and `K: Clone` for
  insert/remove), `V: Clone`, `H: BuildHasher`. The wrapper has **no**
  `BenchMapGetOrInsert`, and its writes are `&mut` even though `horde::SyncTable` is
  internally synchronized. Reads and iteration use `horde::collect::pin`; writes use
  `map.write()`. This is why `horde` is absent from get-or-insert and all concurrent
  groups.
- **`imbl`** (`src/maps/imbl_benchmap.rs`): `H: Default` / `BuildHasher`; `K: Hash +
  Eq` (and `K: Clone` for insert/get-or-insert/remove), `V: Clone`. Clone needs
  `K: Clone` and `H: Clone`. It is a persistent map but the wrapper mutates through
  `&mut`, so it is serial-only. Clear is available.
- **`immutable-chunkmap`** (`src/maps/immutable_chunkmap_benchmap.rs`): `K: Clone +
  Ord`, `V: Clone`; no custom hasher; mutation uses `insert_cow`/`remove_cow`/
  `get_or_insert_cow` through `&mut self`. There is **no** clear implementation. It
  therefore appears only in out-of-the-box groups (and clone/clone-then-write).
- **`indexmap`** (`src/maps/indexmap_benchmap.rs`): `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`,
  `H: BuildHasher` (default `RandomState`). `new_with_hasher` is supported; clone
  needs `K: Clone`. Writes are `&mut`. Note that the wrapper's `remove` uses
  `swap_remove`, which does not preserve insertion order after a removal; that is a
  semantic difference from a shift-remove and matters if iteration order must remain
  stable.
- **`intmap`** (`src/maps/intmap_benchmap.rs`): `K: IntKey`, `V: Clone` for read/
  get-or-insert. There is no hasher parameter at all, so it cannot enter same-hasher
  or key-sensitivity groups, and it is only meaningful for integer keys. The wrapper
  is `&mut`-based and has clear.
- **`leapfrog`** (`src/maps/leapfrog_benchmap.rs`): `K: Eq + Hash + Copy`,
  `V: leapfrog::Value`, `H: BuildHasher + Default`. Writes are available through
  `&self`. There is no clone or clear implementation, and no `String` support because
  of `Copy`. It only enters `u64` groups; this is the key constraint behind its 24
  applicable concurrent groups.
- **`papaya`** (`src/maps/papaya_benchmap.rs`): `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone` for
  value-returning paths, `H: BuildHasher`. Shared-reference operations call
  `map.pin()` internally; clone additionally needs `K: Clone`. Clear is available.
  No unusual ergonomic requirement beyond pinning being hidden by the wrapper.
- **`rpds-hash-trie-map`** (`src/maps/rpds_benchmap.rs`): a persistent
  `rpds::HashTrieMap<K, V, archery::RcK, H>`; `K: Eq + Hash`, `H: BuildHasher +
  Clone`, `V: Clone` for value-returning paths. There is **no** clear or
  get-or-insert wrapper, and mutation is `&mut` (`insert_mut`/`remove_mut`). Clone
  itself does not require `K: Clone`, which is consistent with structural sharing.
- **`rustc-hash`** (`src/maps/rustc_hash_benchmap.rs`): a fixed `FxHashMap`; `K: Hash
  + Eq`, `V: Clone` for read/get-or-insert, clone needs `K: Clone`. There is no
  hasher parameter, so it cannot enter same-hasher or key-sensitivity groups. Writes
  are `&mut`, so it is serial-only. Clear is available.
- **`scc`** (`src/maps/scc_benchmap.rs`): `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`, `H:
  BuildHasher`. The wrapper uses the interior-mutability APIs (`read_sync`,
  `upsert_sync`, `entry_sync`, `remove_sync`, `iter_sync`, `clear_sync`), so
  shared-reference writes are supported. Clone needs `K: Clone`.
- **`starshard`** (`src/maps/starshard_benchmap.rs`): the heaviest bounds in the set:
  `K: Clone + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync`, `H: BuildHasher +
  Clone + Send + Sync`. The default hasher is `rustc_hash::FxBuildHasher`; shared-
  reference writes and get-or-insert are supported, and clone needs the same bounds.
- **`std`** (`src/maps/std_benchmap.rs`): `H: BuildHasher + Default` (or
  `BuildHasher` for `new_with_hasher`), `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`. Clone needs
  `K: Clone`; writes are `&mut`, so it is serial-only. Clear is available.
- **`txmap`** (`src/maps/txmap_benchmap.rs`): `H: BuildHasher` and a
  `txmap::MutexPolicy` map; get-or-insert requires `K: Clone + Hash + Eq`, insert/
  remove require `K: Hash + Eq`, and value-returning paths require `V: Clone`.
  `new_with_hasher` and shared-reference writes are supported; clone needs `K: Clone`.
- **Excluded: `concread`** (`src/maps/concread_benchmap.rs`): `K: Clone + Debug +
  Hash + Eq + Send + Sync + 'static`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync + 'static`. Reads take
  a read guard; writes take a write guard and must call `commit()`. No clone, no
  get-or-insert, no custom hasher. The bench sources comment it out as too slow.
- **Excluded: `concurrent-map`** (`src/maps/concurrent_map_benchmap.rs`): `K:
  'static + Clone + Minimum + Send + Sync`, `V: 'static + Clone + Send + Sync`. The
  crate is `Send` but not `Sync`; the wrapper keeps a master behind a `Mutex` and
  lazily clones it into a thread-local map per iteration. It has no clear and no
  get-or-insert, and it cannot take the common hasher. It is not in the results.
- **Excluded: `flurry`** (`src/maps/flurry_benchmap.rs`): `K: Sync + Send + Clone +
  Hash + Ord`, `V: Sync + Send + Clone`, `H: BuildHasher + Clone`. It has no
  get-or-insert, and the benches comment it out as too slow. It supports
  `new_with_hasher` but requires both `Hash` and `Ord`, which is more restrictive
  than the hash-only or order-only wrappers.

### 5.3 What the benchmark does not measure

- **No memory usage.** The report cannot rank resident size, allocation count, or
  memory amplification.
- **No tail latency.** Criterion medians/means are central-tendency measurements;
  p95/p99/p999 behavior is not available.
- **No baseline or change data.** There is no statistically tested before/after
  comparison and no regression/improvement claim.
- **Limited concurrency scale.** Only 2 and 3 threads are measured; the report does
  not establish behavior at 4, 8, or higher thread counts.
- **Limited concurrent map size.** Concurrent groups use a 10,000-entry map; the
  serial groups cover 1K–1M for selected operations.
- **Limited key shapes.** Most groups use `u64` or exactly 32-byte `String` keys.
  `key-sensitivity` covers `String<16>` and `String<128>` only under a common hasher.
- **No adversarial hash collisions.** Key sets are generated randomly/uniformly or
  from a Zipfian distribution; collision-hardening behavior is not tested.
- **No mixed concurrent resize workload.** Concurrent benchmarks use a pre-populated
  10K map; growth is measured serially.
- **No OS/hardware normalization across machines.** The report describes this run
  only; absolute times should not be transplanted to another machine.

### 5.4 Fairness notes

The benchmark design deliberately separates two questions:

1. **Out-of-the-box:** each map uses its own default hasher and configuration. This
   answers "what does the crate do as shipped?" and favors maps with fast default
   hashers (notably `rustc-hash` and `ahash`).
2. **Same-hasher:** all capable maps use `CommonHasher` (`ahash::RandomState`). This
   answers "which map design handles the same hashing cost better?" but excludes
   `btreemap`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `immutable-chunkmap`, `intmap`, and
   `rustc-hash`, which cannot accept the common hasher in the wrapper.

Neither mode is universally "fair" because the eligible implementation set differs.
A map that wins out-of-the-box may lose under a shared hasher, and vice versa. The
correct comparison is always within one mode and one operation, as in section 2.

### 5.5 Reading the counts

- A win count of 24/37 for `rustc-hash` means it won 24 of the 37 serial variants it
  entered, not 24 of 64 serial variants overall.
- "Within 10%" includes the winner. `rustc-hash` is within 10% in 26/37 serial
  groups, of which 24 are wins.
- A 0-win implementation can still be a useful close contender: `scc` has no
  concurrent wins but is within 10% in 4/48 groups; `hashbrown` has one serial win
  but is within 10% in 23/64.
- Median slowdown is computed over the implementation's applicable groups and is
  intended as a robustness indicator, not as a substitute for the operation-specific
  tables.

## 6. Recommendations and Further Investigation

### 6.1 Measurements to re-run or strengthen

| Area | Why it needs more work | Suggested follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| 100K `String<32>` growth and 3-thread `String<32>` synchronization | These families contain the winning rows with the highest MAD (roughly 6–7%) and several >10% MAD losers. The winners are directionally clear, but exact margins are less precise. | Re-run with more samples or repeated Criterion invocations; report confidence intervals or min/median/max across repeated runs. |
| 1K clone and clone-then-write | Small absolute times and high variance for `dashmap` (18.35% MAD), `btreemap` (12.03%), and `imbl` (8.31%). | Increase sample size for the small-size clone cases and check whether allocator effects dominate. |

| `clone` / `clone-then-write` | Only out-of-the-box `u64` is measured, and clone-then-write adds exactly 10% new keys. | Add `String` keys, 1M entries, and write-after-clone fractions (1%, 10%, 50%) to map the COW cost curve. |

| Memory and tail behavior | Not measured at all. Medians cannot answer “does this fit?” or “what is the worst-case latency?” | Add allocation/peak-RSS tracking and p95/p99/p999 latency capture. |
| Collision behavior | Key sets are random/uniform or Zipfian; no adversarial collisions. | Add a collision-heavy key set for hash maps, especially for DoS/security-sensitive use. |

### 6.2 Implementations or benchmark definitions to investigate

- **`concread`, `concurrent-map`, and `flurry`:** all three are excluded. If they
  matter for a production decision, fix the comparability issue first: `concread`
  needs a guard/commit-aware workload and has no clone/get-or-insert wrapper;
  `concurrent-map` needs a `Send + Sync` usage pattern rather than a thread-local
  clone per iteration; `flurry` needs a fair run without the “too slow” exclusion and
  a get-or-insert shim. Their exclusion should not be read as a performance verdict.
- **`horde`:** the wrapper is `&mut`-only and lacks get-or-insert, despite the
  underlying table being internally synchronized. A shared-reference wrapper would
  answer whether its removal/growth advantage survives concurrency.
- **`indexmap`:** the wrapper uses `swap_remove`; if stable insertion order after
  removals is required, benchmark `shift_remove` separately. The current iteration
  win is real, but the removal semantics are not equivalent to the other maps.
- **`leapfrog`:** only `u64`/`Copy` keys are measured. Test other `Copy` key types
  and a read-only multi-thread workload at larger thread counts before treating it
  as a general concurrent winner.
- **`intmap`:** only `u64` is measured. Other integer widths and sparse integer
  distributions may behave differently, especially around the map's internal
  representation.
- **`rpds-hash-trie-map`:** the clone result is excellent, but the write-after-clone
  cost is high. A workload with a small, known mutation set per snapshot would show
  whether structural sharing is a net win outside the pure-clone case.
- **`btreemap` and `crossbeam-skiplist`:** both are ordered structures; the current
  workloads do not include range scans, predecessor/successor queries, or ordered
  iteration. Their benchmark results therefore understate their natural use cases.
- **`starshard` and `scc`:** both are concurrent maps with no wins in this data.
  Before excluding them, check whether their intended sharding/contention settings
  differ from the wrapper defaults (the wrapper uses the crate defaults).

### 6.3 Measurement-quality conclusions

- The dataset is internally consistent: every group has a unique fastest median, all
  1,323 medians are present, and no MAD exceeds 20% of its median.
- The main ranking conclusions are robust because they are based on >10% gaps or on
  repeated wins across variants. The fragile conclusions are the 1–5% same-hasher
  lookup/miss differences and the noisy 100K String growth / 3-thread String
  synchronization margins.
- The report should be re-run on the target deployment hardware before a production
  decision. Absolute times are not portable across CPUs, allocators, or kernels, and
  this dataset contains no cross-machine normalization.

## Appendix A. Reproducibility and extraction

- Configuration: `src/config.rs`, `src/constants.rs`.
- Benchmark definitions: `benches/*.rs`.
- Map wrappers and trait bounds: `src/maps/*.rs`.
- Criterion report data: `target/criterion/<group>/<implementation>/new/estimates.json`.
- Primary field: `median.point_estimate` (nanoseconds).
- Fallback: none was needed; all 1,323 measurements had a median point estimate.
- Ratio calculation: `contender_median / min(medians_in_group)`.
- Within-10% calculation: `ratio <= 1.10`.
- Stability: `median_abs_dev.point_estimate / median.point_estimate` and
  `std_dev.point_estimate / mean.point_estimate`.

## Appendix B. Bottom line

For serial workloads, `rustc-hash` is the most frequent winner, but `ahash`,
`hashbrown`, and `std` are close often enough that API and hasher constraints should
drive the choice. `indexmap` is the clear iteration/order specialist, `intmap` is the
integer-key lookup/removal specialist, `horde` is the removal and same-hasher `u64`
growth specialist, and `rpds-hash-trie-map` is the clone/snapshot specialist at the
cost of expensive copy-on-write mutation.

For concurrent workloads, `txmap` is the broad default, `leapfrog` is the `u64`
high-contention specialist, `dashmap` is the `String` 2-thread synchronization
specialist, and `papaya`/`crossbeam-skiplist` are narrow read-only winners. No single
implementation is best across serial, concurrent, key types, and operation mixes;
the operation-specific tables are the decision surface, and the close-contender
counts are the robustness check.
