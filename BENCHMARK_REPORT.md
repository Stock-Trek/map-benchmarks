**Important interpretation notes**

- The wrapper contract returns cloned values on lookup (`get_cloned`), and the benchmarks use `u64` values. Value-clone cost is therefore present but deliberately small and consistent across implementations.
- Some rows contain only 100 operations (`lookup-hit`, `lookup-miss`, `remove`) or 100 lookups (`key-sensitivity`). A sub-microsecond median can move by several percent from a handful of samples; use the ratios and §4 stability data together.
- Coverage is not equal. For example, `intmap` appears in 25 serial groups rather than 64 because it cannot accept String keys or a custom hasher. Raw "number of wins" therefore favours implementations that can enter more rows.

### Per-operation takeaways

- **Create:** `immutable-chunkmap` is the clear winner at 2.624 µs for 10,000 empty-map constructions. The next fastest is `rustc-hash` at 4.064 µs (1.55×), but its measurement is noisy (§4). Eagerly allocated or sharded concurrent maps are much more expensive to construct: `papaya` 5.328 ms, `starshard` 2.478 ms, `txmap` 2.324 ms, and `dashmap` 1.633 ms for the same batch.
- **Insert:** `rustc-hash` wins both OOTB rows and is more than 10% ahead. With a common hasher, `hashbrown`, `ahash`, and `std` are effectively tied for `u64` (within 0.6%), while `ahash` and `hashbrown` lead the String row and `std`/`indexmap` are 6.2% behind.
- **Lookup hit:** `intmap` is the clear `u64` winner at 0.117 µs per 100-lookup batch; `rustc-hash` is the clear String winner at 0.494 µs. With a common hasher the field compresses: `hashlink`, `hashbrown`, `std`, and `ahash` are all within 3.8% for `u64`; `std`, `hashbrown`, `ahash`, and `hashlink` are within 6.2% for String.
- **Lookup miss:** the same pattern repeats. `intmap` wins `u64` at 0.085 µs; `rustc-hash` wins String at 0.409 µs. Same-hasher `u64` is a near tie between `hashbrown` and `hashlink` (0.4%); same-hasher String is led by `ahash`, with `std`, `hashbrown`, and `hashlink` within 2.6%.
- **Remove:** `horde` wins three of the four remove rows: 4.396 µs OOTB String, 3.076 µs same-hasher String, and 0.389 µs same-hasher `u64`. `intmap` wins the remaining OOTB `u64` row at 0.340 µs.
- **Get-or-insert:** `rustc-hash` wins both OOTB rows, with `hashbrown` 4.2% behind for String. With a common hasher, `std` and `ahash` tie for String (0.1% apart) and `ahash` leads `u64`, with `std` 0.2% and `hashbrown` 6.4% behind.
- **Growth:** `rustc-hash` wins every OOTB row. With a common hasher, `indexmap` wins every String row, while `std` wins the 1K/10K `u64` rows and `hashbrown` wins 100K/1M `u64`; the top group is typically within 1–8%. At 1M String entries, `rustc-hash` takes 115.176 ms and `indexmap` is 6.9% behind; at 1M `u64`, `rustc-hash` takes 31.453 ms and `hashbrown` is 5.1% behind.
- **Clear and reuse:** `rustc-hash` wins all three OOTB rows. With a common hasher, `std` wins 1K/10K, and `ahash` wins 100K, with `std` and `hashbrown` within 1.1%. `rustc-hash` is more than 10% ahead of the next OOTB map at 10K and 1K; at 100K, `ahash` is 9.3% behind.
- **Iterate:** `indexmap` wins all six rows by a large margin; no other implementation is within 10%. The gap is the clearest structural result in the report: 2.599 µs for 10K entries and 50.177 µs for 100K entries OOTB.
- **Clone:** `rpds-hash-trie-map` wins all three sizes at 0.00104 µs (about 1.04 ns). No other map is within 10%, but the next persistent maps are also cheap: `imbl` at 11.2 ns and `immutable-chunkmap` at 12.8 ns, roughly 10.8× and 12.3× the rpds result. Deep-copy maps scale with size: at 100K, `std` is 150.388 µs and `papaya` is 7.073 ms. `starshard` also reports about 91 ns at every size, but that is a shallow `Arc`-backed handle clone sharing the same shards, not an independent snapshot.
- **Clone then write:** `rustc-hash` wins all three sizes; `ahash` is 4.6% behind at 100K and no other map is within 10%. Persistent maps lose their clone advantage once writes follow: at 100K, `rpds-hash-trie-map` takes 4.183 ms and `immutable-chunkmap` 3.047 ms, versus 0.233 ms for `rustc-hash`.
- **Key sensitivity:** `hashbrown` wins `u64` and `String<128>`, and `hashlink` wins `String<16>`, but the field is tight. The largest gap to a close contender is 6.2% (`std` for `String<16>`); `hashlink` is within 0.2% for `u64`. Longer keys cost more: at 100 lookups, the winning `String<128>` time is 1.462 µs versus 0.216 µs for `u64`.
- **Serial throughput:** `rustc-hash` wins five of six serial rows, by more than 10% in three of them. The exception is String write-heavy, where `hashbrown` wins and `rustc-hash`, `ahash`, and `indexmap` are all within 3.7%.
- **Contention:** `leapfrog` wins every `u64` row by more than 10%. String contention splits: `txmap` wins both Uniform rows (with `scc` within 0.5%), `scc` wins Zipfian(1.0) and 2-thread Zipfian(2.0), and `dashmap` wins 3-thread Zipfian(2.0) with `scc` 0.3% behind.
- **Synchronization:** `leapfrog` wins nine of twelve `u64` rows; `txmap` wins the remaining `u64` write-only/write-mostly rows. For String, `papaya` wins both read-only rows, `txmap` wins most mixed rows, and `dashmap` wins 3-thread write-mostly/write-only. This is the operation with the most high-variance results (§4).
- **Concurrent throughput:** `leapfrog` wins all four `u64` read-heavy/balanced rows. `scc` wins both `u64` write-heavy rows (`txmap` is within 3.1–4.8%; `leapfrog` is within 4.8% at 2 threads), and both String write-heavy rows. `papaya` wins the String read-heavy rows; `scc` wins String balanced, with `txmap` within 2.0% at 3 threads.

### 3.4 Trade-offs and consistently close implementations

- **Hasher control is the largest confounder.** `rustc-hash`'s 25 wins are all in OOTB rows because it cannot accept the common hasher. Once the hasher is held constant, `ahash`, `hashbrown`, and `std` win or tie most hash-map rows. The data therefore supports two different conclusions: "use rustc-hash when its default Fx hasher is acceptable" and "the underlying hash tables are closely matched when hashing is equalised."
- **Iteration versus point operations.** `indexmap` is the iteration specialist (all six wins, no close contender) but has a median ratio of 1.474 across its 64 serial appearances. It should not be selected for point-operation speed on the strength of its iteration result.
- **Clone semantics versus write cost.** Persistent maps (`rpds`, `imbl`, `immutable-chunkmap`) make cloning nearly free but move cost into the first write. The clone-then-write results show this reversal clearly. `rpds` also uses `archery::RcK` and is not `Send + Sync`, so it is best suited to read-only snapshots on one thread.
- **Key-type specialisation.** `intmap` and `leapfrog` are excellent within their constraints, but those constraints are material: integer-only keys for `intmap`, `Copy` keys and no clone/clear for `leapfrog`. They should not be treated as general-purpose replacements.
- **Concurrent results depend on key type and access pattern.** `leapfrog` dominates `u64`; String workloads split among `txmap` (Uniform/mixed), `scc` (Zipfian/write-heavy), `papaya` (read-heavy/read-only), and `dashmap` (high-skew 3-thread writes). There is no "fastest concurrent map" in this dataset.
- **Construction versus runtime.** `immutable-chunkmap` is cheapest to construct, while sharded/eager concurrent maps are expensive to construct but can amortise that setup over concurrent work. `create` should not be used to infer steady-state throughput.
- **Consistently close, but not dominant.** `ahash` and `hashbrown` each finish within 10% in 24 of 64 serial groups; `std` does so in 19. In the concurrent set, `txmap` is within 10% in 21 of 48 groups, and `leapfrog` in 21 of 24 (but only `u64`). No implementation is close everywhere, and raw win counts reward broader API coverage.

## 4. Stability

Most results are stable: across all 1,323 measurements, the median relative MAD is **0.77%**, and 52 measurements (**3.9%**) exceed 5%. The median of each group's maximum relative MAD is **2.54%**. | `leapfrog` | `K: Eq + Hash + Copy`; `V: Value` (the crate trait implies `Copy`); `H: BuildHasher + Default`. | No wrapper clone; no `BenchMapMutClear`; no non-`Copy` keys. | Concurrent internal mutability via `&self`. `K: Copy` excludes `String`. No clone rows and no clear rows. `new` starts empty. |
| `papaya` | `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`, plus `Default`/`Clone` where required; cloning adds `K: Clone`. | No missing benchmark traits in the wrapper. | Every operation pins the map (`self.map.pin()`). Internal mutability and custom hasher are supported. Cloning is a deep copy in this wrapper. |
| `rpds-hash-trie-map` | `K: Eq + Hash`; `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher + Clone`, plus `Default` for `new`. | No get-or-insert; no `BenchMapMutClear`; no `&self` mutation. | Uses `HashTrieMap<K, V, archery::RcK, H>`; `RcK` makes it non-`Send`/non-`Sync`. Insert/remove use `insert_mut`/`remove_mut` behind `&mut self`. Clone is O(1) structural sharing; writes after clone are expensive. |
| `rustc-hash` | `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone`; `K: Clone` for cloning. | No custom hasher and no `new_with_hasher`; no `&self` mutation. | Fixed Fx hasher: very fast but not randomised and not DoS-resistant. Deep clone. Serial-only in this harness. |
| `scc` | `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`, plus `Default`/`Clone` where required. | No missing benchmark traits in the wrapper. | Internal mutability: insert uses `upsert_sync`, reads use `read_sync`, removal uses `remove_sync`, iteration uses `iter_sync`. Custom hasher supported. |
| `starshard` | `K: Clone + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync`; `V: Clone + Send + Sync`; `H: BuildHasher + Clone + Send + Sync`, plus `Default` for `new`. | No missing benchmark traits in the wrapper. | Heavy bounds: keys and values must be `Clone + Send + Sync`, and the hasher must also be `Send + Sync`. Internal mutability and custom hasher supported. |
| `std` | `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`, plus `Default`/`Clone` where required. | No `&self` mutation; no concurrent support. | Default `RandomState` is SipHash, which is DoS-resistant but slower OOTB. Deep clone. Custom hasher supported. |
| `txmap` | `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`, plus `Default`/`Clone` where required; get-or-insert adds `K: Clone`. | No missing benchmark traits in the wrapper. | Wrapper fixes `txmap::MutexPolicy`; shard/capacity/locking-policy tuning is not exposed. Internal mutability and custom hasher supported. Clone is a deep copy. |


**Per-implementation wrapper notes**

- **`ahash`** — Needs `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`, and a hasher with `Default`/`Clone` as appropriate. Only `&mut self` mutation is exposed, so it is serial-only here. Default hashing is randomised.
- **`btreemap`** — Needs `K: Ord`; has no `new_with_hasher` and no shared-reference mutation. Ordered semantics are the reason to choose it, not point-operation speed.
- **`crossbeam-skiplist`** — Needs `K: Ord` (plus `Send + 'static` for shared-reference mutation) and has no wrapper clone or custom hasher. It is the concurrent ordered option, not a general hash-map substitute.
- **`dashmap`** — Implements the full benchmark surface, including shared-reference mutation and custom hashers. Its wrapper clone deep-copies shards; it does not share structure.
- **`hashbrown`** — Implements the serial surface with a configurable hasher, but has no shared-reference mutation. Clone is a deep copy.
- **`hashlink`** — Implements the serial surface and maintains insertion order through a linked list. Clone is O(n), and there is no concurrent mutation.
- **`horde`** — Has no get-or-insert. Reads require `horde::collect::pin`, and writes go through `write()`; `clear` is emulated with an empty-map replacement. Mutation is `&mut self` only.
- **`imbl`** — A persistent HAMT with cheap structural-sharing clones. Mutations require `&mut self` and add `K: Clone`; there is no shared-reference mutation.
- **`immutable-chunkmap`** — Needs `K: Clone + Ord`; has no custom hasher and no wrapper clear. Writes use copy-on-write methods behind `&mut self`, so clone-then-write is expensive.
- **`indexmap`** — Implements the serial surface and owns the iteration benchmarks. Removal uses `swap_remove`, so it does not preserve insertion order unless the caller deliberately uses a shift-remove strategy.
- **`intmap`** — Integer keys only (`K: IntKey`); no custom hasher and no String keys. All mutations are `&mut self`, and clone is a deep copy.
- **`leapfrog`** — Needs `K: Copy` and `V: Value` (which implies `Copy`); it has no wrapper clone and no clear. It supports shared-reference mutation and custom hashers for `u64` keys, but cannot participate in String-key rows.
- **`papaya`** — Implements the full surface, but every operation pins the map via `self.map.pin()`. The wrapper clone is a deep copy, not structural sharing.
- **`rpds-hash-trie-map`** — Uses `archery::RcK`, so it is not `Send`/`Sync`; mutations use `&mut self` and mutating trie methods. It has no get-or-insert or clear. Clone is O(1), but writes after clone are expensive.
- **`rustc-hash`** — Fixes the Fx hasher, so there is no `new_with_hasher`; serial-only in this harness. Fast, but not randomised or DoS-resistant. Clone is a deep copy.
- **`scc`** — Implements the full surface with shared-reference mutation via `upsert_sync`, `read_sync`, `remove_sync`, and `iter_sync`. Custom hashers are supported.
- **`starshard`** — Implements the full surface but imposes heavy bounds: keys and values must be `Clone + Send + Sync`, and the hasher must also be `Send + Sync`. Its `clone` is a shallow `Arc` handle clone that shares the same shards (about 91 ns at every size), not an independent copy; consequently its `clone-then-write` row is not a copy-on-write comparison.
- **`std`** — Implements the serial surface with a configurable hasher. The default `RandomState` uses SipHash, which is slower out of the box but DoS-resistant. Clone is a deep copy.
- **`txmap`** — Implements the full surface and supports custom hashers, but the wrapper fixes `txmap::MutexPolicy`; shard count, capacity, and locking-policy tuning are not exposed. Clone is a deep copy.

### 5.2 Wrappers present but not benchmarked

| Implementation | Why it is outside the Criterion results |
|---|---|
| `concread` | Requires `K: Clone + Debug + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync + 'static` and `V: Clone + Send + Sync + 'static`. The wrapper has no clone, no get-or-insert, and no custom hasher. Mutations use a write guard and an explicit `commit()` per operation. The repository documentation reports it as too slow for the benchmark set. |
| `concurrent-map` | Requires `K: Clone + Minimum + Send + Sync + 'static` and `V: Clone + Send + Sync + 'static`. The map is `Send` but not `Sync`; the wrapper works around this with a mutex plus a thread-local clone per worker, which changes what is being measured. It also lacks a custom hasher, get-or-insert, and clear. The repository documentation says the workaround corrupts timings, so it is excluded. |
| `flurry` | Requires `K: Hash + Ord + Send + Sync + Clone` and `V: Send + Sync` (with `V: Clone` for reads/removal). Access requires pinning (`pin()`). The wrapper has no get-or-insert. The repository documentation reports it as too slow for the benchmark set. |

### 5.3 Cross-cutting wrapper restrictions

- Every benchmark read uses `get_cloned`, so implementations must clone `V`. The benchmarks use `u64` values to keep this constant and small; results for large or expensive-to-clone values may differ.
- The wrapper's shared-reference traits cover only `get`, `insert`, `remove`, and `get_or_insert`. An implementation that exposes a different concurrent API (for example, requiring guards or pinning) needs a wrapper adaptation, and that adaptation can dominate the measurement.
- `clear` is not universal. `immutable-chunkmap`, `leapfrog`, and `rpds-hash-trie-map` do not implement the wrapper's clear trait, so they are absent from clear-and-reuse. `concurrent-map` also lacks clear.
- Get-or-insert is not universal. `horde`, `rpds-hash-trie-map`, and the three unbenchmarked wrappers do not implement it, so their absence from those rows is an API gap rather than a performance result.
- Ordered implementations (`btreemap`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `immutable-chunkmap`) require `Ord` rather than `Hash`, so they cannot participate in same-hasher comparisons. This is a semantic difference, not just a performance difference.

## 6. Recommendations

The data supports the following conclusions and next steps.

### 6.1 Conclusions

1. **There is no single fastest map.** The winner changes with operation, key type, concurrency, and whether the default hasher is part of the decision.
2. **For serial hash-map work, `rustc-hash` is the most frequent OOTB winner**, but a large part of that advantage is its default Fx hasher. With `CommonHasher`, `ahash`, `hashbrown`, and `std` are usually within a few percent and often faster on individual rows.
3. **Specialists are real and large.** `indexmap` owns iteration, `rpds-hash-trie-map` owns cheap cloning, `intmap` owns integer-key point operations, and `leapfrog` owns concurrent `u64` workloads. Those advantages disappear or reverse outside their constraints.
4. **Concurrent String workloads have no dominant map.** `txmap`, `scc`, `papaya`, and `dashmap` each win distinct access patterns; the right choice depends on read/write mix and skew.
5. **Treat sub-10% differences as ties**, especially in write-heavy concurrent synchronization and very short lookup batches. The measured variance in those rows is comparable to the differences between adjacent implementations.

### 6.2 Further investigation

- **Validate finalists on production-like data.** The benchmark clones `u64` values and uses fixed key sizes. Re-run the shortlist with the real value type, key length distribution, and map sizes, especially if values are expensive to clone.
- **Resolve the concurrent String choice with a workload trace.** There is no dominant concurrent String map: `txmap`, `scc`, `papaya`, and `dashmap` win different mixes. Capture the real read/write ratio and key-frequency skew, then test only the two or three candidates that match it.
- **Re-test clone-then-write with the real write fraction.** The benchmark inserts new entries equal to 10% of the map size. Persistent maps are attractive at 0% writes and become much less attractive as writes increase; the crossover should be measured for the intended workload.
- **Decide the hashing policy before choosing a map.** If DoS resistance or hash randomisation is required, exclude `rustc-hash` and re-rank the same-hasher results. If it is not required, `rustc-hash` is a strong default.
- **Investigate the excluded wrappers only if they are candidates.** `concread`, `concurrent-map`, and `flurry` were excluded for slow performance or wrapper workarounds that distort timing. Revisit them only after fixing those integration issues; their absence is not evidence that they are unusable in their native APIs.
- **Revisit high-variance rows with more samples.** `create`, short lookup batches, and write-heavy concurrent synchronization would benefit from additional repetitions or a dedicated harness before a close ranking is treated as settled.

### 6.3 Measurement quality issues

- **No baseline or change data.** This report contains absolute measurements from one Criterion run set, not before/after comparisons. It cannot establish regressions or improvements over time. The `base/` outputs in the tree mirror `new/` (all 1,323 median estimates match), so they are not historical baselines.
- **One machine, one environment.** Worker pinning reduces scheduling noise, but CPU model, core count, memory bandwidth, and turbo behaviour still affect results. Rankings should be confirmed on the deployment target.
- **Medians hide tail behaviour.** A map with a good median can still have unacceptable p99 latency. Criterion's raw sample data would be needed for a tail-latency decision.
- **Short batches amplify relative noise.** `lookup-hit`, `lookup-miss`, `remove`, and `key-sensitivity` measure only 100 operations, so sub-microsecond differences should not be over-interpreted.
- **Some derived per-element numbers are below timer resolution.** The fastest clone medians are around 1 ns, and the fastest `create` batch implies roughly 0.26 ns per empty-map construction. Treat `rpds-hash-trie-map`, `imbl`, and `immutable-chunkmap` as all effectively O(1) for snapshot use, and avoid ranking maps on differences of a few nanoseconds per construction.
- **Coverage is uneven.** Implementations that support more key types and hashers appear in more rows. Raw win counts are useful for pattern-finding but are not a fair normalised score.
- **The hasher is a confounder in OOTB rows.** `rustc-hash` cannot use `CommonHasher`, so its OOTB wins and the same-hasher rankings answer different questions. Both are reported, but they should not be combined into a single league table.
- **A few means are outlier-dominated.** The median is the primary statistic for this reason. Several mean ratios are extremely large because of rare slow groups, and they should not be used to rank implementations.
