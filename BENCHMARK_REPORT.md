### 3.3 Trade-offs

- **Single-threaded vs concurrent:** the fastest serial maps (rustc-hash, std, ahash, hashbrown) are not in the concurrent top tier, and the fastest concurrent maps (txmap, leapfrog, scc) are 2–10× slower than serial winners when used from one thread (e.g. txmap balanced/100K/1t is 187 µs vs rustc-hash 68 µs).
- **Iteration vs everything else:** indexmap is mid-pack on insert (~1.8× slower than rustc-hash at 100K) but uniquely fast at iteration; it also pays for `swap_remove` semantics.
- **Clone semantics:** persistent maps (rpds, immutable-chunkmap, imbl) clone in nanoseconds but are the slowest on insert/get-or-insert; deep-cloning maps (papaya, hashlink) are fastest to *use* but very expensive to clone.
- **Hasher is a first-order effect:** rustc-hash's out-of-the-box dominance is substantially its FxHasher, not its table. Under a common hasher, std/ahash/hashbrown match it within ~10–30% depending on operation, and `std`/`ahash` actually beat it on some ops (e.g. remove at 100K, lookup-miss at 10K).

### 3.4 Consistently close implementations

- **hashbrown**: within 10% of the fastest in 24/58 groups — the most consistent all-rounder; never far behind anywhere it runs.
- **ahash** (18/58) and **std** (16/58): same underlying hashbrown table; essentially interchangeable.
- **txmap**: within 10% in 28/94 and the winner in 26 — consistently at or near the top of every concurrent group.
- **leapfrog**: within 10% in 13/83 groups and a frequent concurrent winner; its noise (see §5) makes it look less consistent than it is.

## 4. Stability

Stability is assessed via `median_abs_dev` (MAD) relative to the median. Criterion's MAD is the robust analogue of the standard deviation; a MAD/median above ~10% indicates visibly noisy measurements.

### 4.1 Overall picture

- **Serial benchmarks are extremely stable.** Winners in out-of-the-box and same-hasher serial groups typically show MAD/median < 3% (e.g. rustc-hash clear-and-reuse 0.4–0.7%, insert 0.2–0.3%, std same-hasher clear-and-reuse 0.4–0.7%).
- **Concurrent benchmarks are much noisier**, especially at 4 threads and 100K entries. This is expected for multi-threaded wall-clock measurements (scheduling, cache-line contention, allocator pressure), but it means small winner margins in §2.6/§2.13 should not be over-interpreted.

### 4.3 Fast implementations with unusually noisy measurements

- **txmap (winner) in 4-thread groups:** get-or-insert/100K/4t MAD 43.9%; workload balanced/100K/4t 28.6%; balanced/10K/4t 16.5%; read-heavy/10K/4t 16.1%. Its 4-thread wins should be read with caution.
- **leapfrog (winner) in 4-thread groups:** get-or-insert/1K/4t MAD 10.3%; balanced/1K/4t 11.0%; write-heavy/10K/4t 10.0%. More dramatically, read-heavy/100K/4t has MAD only 3.6% but std/med 111% — a stable core with a heavy tail of very slow iterations, i.e. occasional multi-millisecond samples. Its wins over txmap at 4 threads are within the noise band.
- **dashmap / scc / papaya** in 4-thread/100K groups are the noisiest of the concurrent set (MAD up to 49% / 45%; papaya read-heavy/100K/4t std/med 98%). They are not winners, so this mostly affects how close they appear to be.
- **rpds-hash-trie-map** is noisy even in serial workloads (MAD 36–46% at 100K) — likely allocation behavior of the persistent trie. Not a winner there, but its slow/steady medians in those groups are only approximate.
- **clone (rpds, 2 ns):** MAD/median 16.2% at 100K is a quantization artifact of measuring a 2 ns constant; not meaningful.
- **iterate same-hasher/100K (indexmap, winner):** MAD 16.5% but still 3.6× faster than the next — the conclusion is unaffected.

### 4.4 Measurement-quality caveats

- Concurrent groups have only 7 implementations (the concurrent-capable ones); serial 1-thread workload groups have all 19. Comparisons across groups with different participant sets are still valid per-group (fastest within that group).
- `get-or-insert` serial runs only 200 operations per iteration; the sub-µs medians are robust (MAD ≤3%) but the operation mix is tiny.
- 4-thread winner margins under ~5–10% (e.g. txmap vs leapfrog) are within the measured noise and should be treated as ties.

## 5. Implementation Limitations (from `src/maps/` wrappers)

General notes: `V: Clone` is required by almost every wrapper's `get_cloned`/`get_or_insert` because the benchmark API returns owned values. "same-hasher" participation requires `BenchMapNewWithHasher`. `concread` and `flurry` are wrapped but excluded from all benchmarks (too slow per bench comments).

- **ahash** (`AhashBenchMap`): requires `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone` for get/get-or-insert. Supports custom hasher (`BenchMapNewWithHasher`). Present in both modes.
- **std** (`StdBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone` for get/get-or-insert. Default hasher `RandomState` (SipHash-1-3). Supports custom hasher. No unusual restrictions.
- **rustc-hash** (`RustCHashBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq` for most ops; **no custom-hasher API** (fixed FxBuildHasher) — excluded from all same-hasher benchmarks and key-sensitivity. FxHasher is fast but not DoS-resistant. Note the create bench needs no bounds at all (fastest create after immutable-chunkmap).
- **hashbrown** (`HashbrownBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone` for get/get-or-insert. Supports custom hasher. Default hasher is foldhash (`DefaultHashBuilder`). No unusual restrictions.
- **hashlink** (`HashlinkBenchMap`): `LinkedHashMap` — insertion-ordered. `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone` for get/get-or-insert. Supports custom hasher. Clone is a deep, expensive copy (5.78 ms at 100K).
- **btreemap** (`BTreeMapBenchMap`): `K: Ord` (ordered map); `V: Clone`. **No custom-hasher API** (excluded from same-hasher) — appropriate, it is not hash-based. Not in concurrent benches.
- **horde** (`HordeBenchMap`, `SyncTable`): requires `K: Clone + Hash + Eq` and `V: Clone` for insert/get-or-insert — **values are cloned on every read path** and on removal (`.map(|tuple| tuple.1.clone())`). `get_or_insert` is emulated as a read-then-write (not a single atomic entry op). Reads require `horde::collect::pin`. Clear is implemented via `write().replace(empty, 0)`. Supports custom hasher. Present in both modes; this is the remove specialist under a common hasher.
- **indexmap** (`IndexMapBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone`. Supports custom hasher. **Remove uses `swap_remove`** (O(1), but reorders entries — iteration order is not preserved; the remove bench measures only the removal cost). Iteration is the fastest of all implementations due to contiguous storage.
- **leapfrog** (`LeapfrogBenchMap`, `LeapMap`): **`K: Copy + Eq + Hash`** and **`V: leapfrog::Value` (Sized + Debug + Clone + Copy)** — values must be `Copy`. **No `Clone` impl** (excluded from clone bench) and **no `clear`** (excluded from clear-and-reuse). Supports custom hasher; provides `&self` (concurrent-style) insert/remove/get-or-insert via interior mutability. Present in all other benches including concurrent.
- **papaya** (`PapayaBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone` for get/remove/get-or-insert. Every operation goes through `map.pin()` (hazard-pointer guard). **Clone is deep and very expensive** (8.63 ms at 100K, slowest of all). Supports custom hasher; concurrent.
- **scc** (`SccBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone`. Uses `entry_sync`/`upsert_sync`/`iter_sync`/`clear_sync` (sync variants of an async-friendly API). Supports custom hasher; concurrent.
- **starshard** (`StarshardBenchMap`, `ShardedHashMap`): **`K: Clone + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync`** and **`V: Clone + Send + Sync`** — values must be `Clone` (get returns a clone), keys cloned on insert. Fixed 8 shards (`with_shards_and_hasher(8, …)`). `compute_if_absent` for get-or-insert. Supports custom hasher; concurrent.
- **txmap** (`TxMapBenchMap`, `TxMap` with `MutexPolicy`): **`K: Clone + Hash + Eq`**; `V: Clone` for get-or-insert. Constructed via a **builder** (`TxMapBuilder`). Uses `get_with_or_insert` (transactional). Supports custom hasher; concurrent. Most frequent overall winner.
- **imbl** (`ImblBenchMap`, `GenericHashMap` with `DefaultSharedPtr`): **`K: Hash + Eq + Clone`**, `V: Clone`. Persistent/immutable (structural sharing) — **clone is O(1)** (4–5 ns). Insert/get-or-insert require `V: Clone`. Supports custom hasher. Not in concurrent benches.
- **immutable-chunkmap** (`ImmutableChunkMapBenchMap`, `MapM`): **`K: Clone + Ord`** (ordered, no hashing) and `V: Clone`. Persistent map with COW (`insert_cow`, `remove_cow`, `get_or_insert_cow`). **No `clear`** (excluded from clear-and-reuse), **no custom-hasher API** (excluded from same-hasher). Not in concurrent benches. Fastest at empty-map creation; clone is O(1).
- **rpds-hash-trie-map** (`RpdsHashTrieMapBenchMap`, `HashTrieMap` with `archery::RcK`): `K: Eq + Hash`; `V: Clone` for get/remove. **No entry API** — get-or-insert is emulated as a get followed by an in-place `insert_mut` (not atomic); **remove is emulated as get + `remove_mut` (two lookups)**. Clone is O(1) (persistent). **No `clear`** (excluded from clear-and-reuse). Supports custom hasher. Not in concurrent benches.
- **concurrent-map** (`ConcurrentMapBenchMap`, `concurrent_map::ConcurrentMap`): **`K: 'static + Clone + Minimum + Send + Sync`** — `Minimum` is a custom trait requiring `Ord` plus a `const MIN` sentinel value that must be ≤ every possible key (an ergonomic burden). `V: 'static + Clone + Send + Sync`. **No `clear`** (excluded from clear-and-reuse), **no custom hasher**. Clone is cheap (~170 ns, shared structure). Not in same-hasher benches.
- **crossbeam-skiplist** (`CrossbeamSkiplistBenchMap`, `SkipMap`): **`K: Ord + Send + 'static`** (ordered, no hashing), `V: Send + 'static` (+ `Clone` for get/remove/get-or-insert). **No custom hasher**, no `Clone` impl (excluded from clone bench). Concurrent.
- **concread / flurry** (excluded): `ConcreadBenchMap` has no `Clone` impl, needs `K: Debug + Clone + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync + 'static` and takes a write transaction + explicit `commit()` on every mutation. `FlurryBenchMap` requires `K: Sync + Send + Clone + Hash + Ord`. Both were excluded because "too slow" (flurry creates a `seize::Collector` per map).

## 6. Recommendations

### Further investigation

1. **rustc-hash vs hashbrown with a common fast hasher.** rustc-hash's out-of-the-box dominance is partly hasher-driven. A same-hasher run that feeds FxHasher into hashbrown/ahash would separate table performance from hasher performance and settle whether FxHashMap's table adds anything beyond its hasher.
2. **txmap vs leapfrog at 4 threads.** Their margins are frequently inside the measured noise (e.g. get-or-insert/100K/4t: txmap ×1.025 over leapfrog; workload write-heavy/100K/4t: leapfrog wins, scc close at 3t). Longer measurement times and more repetitions would tighten the confidence intervals; pinning/affinity effects and allocator behavior (leapfrog uses `stable_alloc`) should be investigated.
3. **horde's remove advantage.** horde is clearly fastest at remove under a common hasher despite cloning values on every operation — worth understanding which mechanism (lock-free table, deferred reclamation) drives it, and whether it generalises beyond the remove benchmark.
4. **indexmap's swap_remove semantics.** Its iterate dominance is real, but `swap_remove` changes iteration order; users who need order-preserving removal pay more. A follow-up could measure `shift_remove` for the order-preserving variant.
5. **Persistent-map clone semantics.** rpds/immutable-chunkmap/imbl clone in nanoseconds because they share structure; the benchmark reports handle-copy cost. If a deep-copy (snapshot isolation) use case is intended, these numbers do not apply — and papaya/hashlink are the expensive deep-clone outliers worth knowing about.
6. **rpds get-or-insert/remove emulation.** Both are emulated with two operations (get + mutate). A native `entry` API would change its relative position; the current numbers understate what an optimized rpds could do but also overstate what the current API delivers atomically.
7. **String-key scaling.** String lookups cost 3–9× u64 lookups and compress the field (ahash edges ahead). A key-sensitivity suite with more key types (e.g. bytes, small structs) and larger maps would clarify whether this is hashing cost or cache effects.

### Measurement-quality issues affecting conclusions

- **Concurrent 4-thread results are noisy** (MAD up to 87%, std/med up to 111%). Winner-vs-runner-up margins under ~10% in §2.6/§2.13 (txmap vs leapfrog vs scc) should be treated as ties. Prefer 2–3-thread results for rank ordering.
- **leapfrog read-heavy/100K/4t** shows a stable median with an extreme tail (std/med 111%); its median is trustworthy but its distribution is heavy-tailed — investigate occasional multi-millisecond iterations (GC of hazard pointers? allocator stalls?).
- **rpds serial workloads at 100K** have MAD 36–46%: its medians there are approximate.
- **clone (rpds/immutable-chunkmap/imbl) sub-10 ns values** are handle-copy costs; treat any MAD% on them as quantization noise.
- Serial results are very stable (MAD < 3% for winners) and are the most trustworthy part of the dataset; the same-hasher serial groups are the cleanest comparison of map tables in isolation.
