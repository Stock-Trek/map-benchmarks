### How to read this report

* A **benchmark group** is one workload, e.g. `insert/out-of-the-box/u64`, and each map
  contributes one measurement to it.
* "**Fastest**" means the lowest median in that group.
* "**Within 10%**" means the implementation's median is at most `1.10 ×` the fastest
  median — i.e. close enough that the difference is unlikely to matter in practice.
  The ratio `×1.05` means "5% slower than the fastest".
* Times below 1 µs are shown in nanoseconds (ns); larger values in µs, ms or s. All are
  converted from the raw nanoseconds in `estimates.json`.
* Each figure is the benchmark's own median; this report does not re-run anything.

**Column definitions**

* **Internal mutability** — whether the wrapper exposes mutation through a *shared*
  reference (`&self`), i.e. `BenchMapInsert`/`BenchMapGetOrInsert`/`BenchMapRemove`
  rather than the `&mut self` variants. This is what allows a map to be shared across
  threads. ❌ means mutating benchmarks must hold `&mut`, so the map cannot participate
  in the concurrent workloads.
* **Configurable hasher** — the wrapper implements `BenchMapNewWithHasher`, so the
  `same-hasher` comparison can force the common `ahash::RandomState` hasher. ❌ means the
  map either fixes its hasher or does not expose one, and it therefore cannot appear in
  `same-hasher` groups.
* **Non-`Copy` keys** — whether keys such as `String` are supported. `intmap` requires
  `K: IntKey` (integer keys) and `leapfrog` requires `K: Copy`, so both are restricted to
  `u64` keys.

**Note on the three removed implementations.** The README documents that `concread`,
`flurry` and `concurrent_map` were removed before benchmarking: `concread` and `flurry`
were substantially slower than the rest, and `concurrent_map` is `Send` but not `Sync`,
which forced a thread-local workaround that corrupted the timings. They are excluded from
every number in this report. Benchmark source comments additionally record `concread` /
`flurry` as having no `Clone`/entry API, and `concurrent_map` as having no `clear`.

reuse` under `same-hasher`, have clusters of statistically indistinguishable
implementations — for those the choice should be driven by API fit, trait bounds and
stability rather than by the ranking.

### 3.4 Trade-offs between implementations

* **Serial speed vs concurrency.** The implementations that dominate serial work —
  `rustc-hash`, `intmap`, `indexmap`, `horde`, `rpds-hash-trie-map` — cannot be mutated
  through a shared reference and so are absent from every concurrent group. Conversely
  `txmap` is the fastest concurrent map (37/48 concurrent groups) yet is the fastest in
  only 1 of 64 serial groups. There is no single winner across both worlds; the fast path
  and the concurrent path are genuinely different data structures.
* **Cheap clone vs write speed.** The persistent/structural-sharing maps
  (`rpds-hash-trie-map`, `immutable-chunkmap`, `imbl`) clone in nanoseconds but are not
  competitive on insert/lookup/growth. For example `rpds-hash-trie-map` clones a 100K map
  in 1.76 ns versus ~62 µs for a deep-copy map (`rustc-hash`), yet it does not win any
  write operation.
* **Iteration vs write cost.** `indexmap` wins every `iterate` variant and large-`String`
  `growth`, at the cost of maintaining a parallel index vector; it is also not the fastest
  on several write paths.
* **Hasher sensitivity (out-of-the-box vs same-hasher).** Much of `rustc-hash`'s
  out-of-the-box edge comes from its FxHasher, not the table. Once the hasher is held
  constant (`same-hasher`), `std`, `hashbrown` and `ahash` rise to the top of many
  operations. Conclusions about the *data structure* should be drawn from the
  `same-hasher` tables; the `out-of-the-box` tables describe realistic defaults.
* **Key type drives the ranking.** `intmap` and `leapfrog` are competitive only for
  integer/`Copy` keys. Any workload with `String` keys must ignore them, which is why
  several "clearly ahead" results (e.g. `lookup-miss/u64/out-of-the-box`: `intmap` at
  143 ns) do not generalise.

### 3.5 Implementations that are consistently close to the fastest

* **`hashbrown`** — fastest in 3 serial groups but within 10% in **26 of 64** serial
  groups. The single most consistent serial choice.
* **`ahash`** — within 10% in **24 of 64** serial groups.
* **`std`** — within 10% in **20 of 64** serial groups and fastest in 12.
* **`indexmap`** — within 10% in **12 of 64**, fastest in 9 (all `iterate` plus large
  `growth` and one heavy-write throughput variant).
* **`txmap`** (concurrent) — within 10% in **43 of 48** concurrent groups, the clearest
  "consistently near the top" result in the whole suite.

At the other end, `btreemap`, `imbl`, `papaya`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `scc` and
`starshard` are never within 10% of the fastest on **serial** work, and `starshard` and
`crossbeam-skiplist` are never within 10% on **concurrent** work either.

### 4.4 Notes on the noisy results

* **Very small absolute times.** The `rpds-hash-trie-map` `clone` rows have 8–11%
  relative MAD, but the medians are 1.76–1.80 ns. An absolute deviation of a fraction of
  a nanosecond is meaningless in practice; the clone advantage is real and huge.
* **Heavy tails without a high MAD.** `clear-and-reuse` at 1K has `std_dev/median`
  above 60% for `ahash`, `hashbrown` and `rustc-hash` (and `std`'s 10K same-hasher row is
  12.3% MAD). The distributions look bimodal/outlier-prone — most likely allocator or
  pool behaviour — and the medians for those groups should be treated as provisional.
* **`rustc-hash` is the most notable fast-but-noisy implementation.** It is fastest in
  `clear-and-reuse/10K/out-of-the-box` (8.0% MAD), `insert/u64/out-of-the-box` (8.8%),
  and both single-thread `u64` throughput variants (11.0% balanced, 10.0% write-heavy),
  yet its median relative MAD across all 37 rows is only 1.19%. Its wins are genuine but
  its margins in those specific groups are within the noise.
* **Large-map growth is noisy.** `growth` to 1M entries shows 8–15% MAD for `papaya`,
  `imbl`, `crossbeam-skiplist` and `indexmap`. These are hundreds of milliseconds and
  are the most sensitive to allocator/page-fault/THP effects.
* **High `std_dev` with low MAD.** `leapfrog`'s 3-thread `u64` contention rows have
  low MAD (0.15–1.9%) but `std_dev` up to 27.5%, indicating a handful of outlying
  iterations rather than a broad spread. The median remains trustworthy there.

## 5. Implementation Limitations

Findings are drawn from the wrapper code in `src/maps/` (file names are the
implementation identifiers) and from the per-benchmark exclusion comments in `benches/`.

**ahash** (`ahash_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`.
* Configurable hasher: yes (`new_with_hasher`).
* Mutations go through `&mut self` only — no shared-reference API; excluded from all
  concurrent benchmarks.
* Wraps `ahash::AHashMap`; the hasher type is a generic parameter defaulting to
  `ahash::RandomState`.

**btreemap** (`btreemap_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Ord`, `V: Clone`.
* No configurable hasher at all — `same-hasher` groups are impossible, so it appears only
  in out-of-the-box groups (37 groups).
* `&mut self` mutation only; not concurrent.
* Ordered iteration is available and correctly preserved.

**crossbeam-skiplist** (`crossbeam_skiplist_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Ord + Send + 'static`, `V: Clone + Send + 'static` for the mutating and
  get-or-insert paths; read/iterate only need `K: Ord`.
* Supports shared-reference mutation (`insert(&self)`) and a native `get_or_insert`, so
  it runs in all concurrent groups.
* No configurable hasher (it is not hashed at all — skip list keyed by `Ord`), so it has
  **zero** `same-hasher` groups.
* Does **not** implement `Clone`; excluded from the `clone` benchmark.

**dashmap** (`dashmap_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher + Clone`.
* Shared-reference mutation and native `get_or_insert`; configurable hasher.
* `clear` available through `&mut`.
* Sharded locking: reads/writes take a per-shard lock, which is visible in the concurrent
  results.

**hashbrown** (`hashbrown_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq` (plus `K: Clone` for `clone_map`); `H: BuildHasher`.
* Configurable hasher. `&mut self` mutation only; not concurrent.
* Wraps `hashbrown::HashMap` with `hashbrown::DefaultHashBuilder` by default.

**hashlink** (`hashlink_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`.
* Configurable hasher. `&mut self` mutation only.
* Wraps `hashlink::LinkedHashMap`; iteration order is insertion order.

**horde** (`horde_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Clone + Hash + Eq` for insertion, `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`.
* **No entry / get-or-insert API is exposed** — it is excluded from the `get_or_insert`
  benchmark.
* Configurable hasher.
* Despite being a concurrent (`horde::SyncTable`) map, the wrapper only implements the
  `&mut self` variants (`BenchMapMutInsert`, `BenchMapMutRemove`). The benchmark comments
  state explicitly that mutation requires `&mut` here, so it is excluded from all
  concurrent workloads. Reads use a `horde::collect::pin` / `read(pin)` guard; writes use
  a `write()` guard.

**imbl** (`imbl_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq + Clone`, `V: Clone`, `H: BuildHasher + Clone`.
* Configurable hasher. `&mut self` mutation only; the benchmark comments classify it as
  "mutation requires `&mut`, cannot mutate through a shared reference".
* Persistent HAMT: a `clone` is cheap, but the API used for insertion (`insert`) requires
  exclusive ownership.

**immutable-chunkmap** (`immutable_chunkmap_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Clone + Ord`, `V: Clone` — note the `Ord` requirement, unusual for a hash
  map (it is a persistent chunked/ordered structure).
* **No configurable hasher** and **no `clear`** — excluded from `same-hasher` and
  `clear-and-reuse` groups.
* Mutations are copy-on-write (`insert_cow`, `remove_cow`, `get_or_insert_cow`) and
  require `&mut self` / storing the new map; the benchmark comments record this as
  "mutation returns a new map; requires `&mut` or storing the result". Excluded from
  concurrent benchmarks.

**indexmap** (`indexmap_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq` (plus `K: Clone` for `clone_map`); `H: BuildHasher`.
* Configurable hasher. `&mut self` mutation only.
* `remove` is implemented with `swap_remove`, so **removal does not preserve insertion
  order** (and was chosen for speed).

**intmap** (`intmap_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: IntKey` — **integer keys only**; `String` keys are impossible, so it
  appears in `u64` variants only.
* **No configurable hasher** (it is a trie, not hashed).
* `&mut self` mutation only; not concurrent. Tracks the removed value via `get` before
  `remove`.

**leapfrog** (`leapfrog_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Eq + Hash + Copy` — **keys must be `Copy`**, so `String` variants are
  impossible.
* Values must implement leapfrog's `Value` trait; the crate is built with the
  `stable_alloc` feature.
* Shared-reference mutation and a native `get_or_insert` (`try_insert`); configurable
  hasher. Because keys are `Copy`, it appears in `u64` variants of both serial and
  concurrent benchmarks.
* **No `clear`** and **no `Clone`** — excluded from `clear-and-reuse` and `clone`.

**papaya** (`papaya_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`.
* Shared-reference mutation and native `get_or_insert`; configurable hasher.
* Every operation must go through a **pin/guard**: the wrapper calls `self.map.pin()`
  before `get`/`insert`/`get_or_insert`/`iter`/`remove`. This is the ergonomically
  unusual part of the API.
* `clear` requires `&mut self`.

**rpds-hash-trie-map** (`rpds_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Eq + Hash`; `H: BuildHasher + Clone`.
* Configurable hasher.
* Uses the `archery::RcK` (non-thread-safe, `Rc`-based) pointer kind → **not `Send`/
  `Sync`**, and cannot participate in concurrent benchmarks.
* Mutations use `insert_mut`/`remove_mut`; the benchmark comments describe mutation as
  returning a new map / requiring `&mut`. **No `clear`** and **no entry/get-or-insert
  API** — excluded from `clear-and-reuse` and `get_or_insert`.
* Clone is O(1) structural sharing.

**rustc-hash** (`rustc_hash_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq` (plus `K: Clone` for clone); values need `Clone` for get-cloned.
* **No configurable hasher** (it is tied to `FxHasher`), so it has **zero** same-hasher
  groups.
* `&mut self` mutation only; not concurrent — and being non-cryptographic, `FxHasher` is
  not collision-resistant and is unsuitable for adversarial keys.

**scc** (`scc_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; `H: BuildHasher`.
* Shared-reference mutation (`upsert_sync`) and native `get_or_insert`
  (`entry_sync(...).or_insert(...)`); configurable hasher.
* The wrapper uses the blocking `*_sync` methods and `iter_sync`, whose visitor returns
  `bool` to continue iteration.

**starshard** (`starshard_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Clone + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync`,
  `H: BuildHasher + Clone + Send + Sync` — the **heaviest bounds** in the suite; values
  must be `Send + Sync` even for single-threaded use.
* Shared-reference mutation and native `get_or_insert` (`compute_if_absent`, taking a
  closure); configurable hasher.
* `get` returns an owned clone rather than a reference.

**std** (`std_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq` (plus `K: Clone` for clone); `H: BuildHasher`.
* Configurable hasher; default SipHash (`RandomState`). `&mut self` mutation only.
* This is the baseline `std::collections::HashMap` (SwissTable).

**txmap** (`txmap_benchmap.rs`)
* Bounds: `K: Hash + Eq` (plus `K: Clone` for the get-or-insert/ clone paths),
  `V: Clone` for get-or-insert; `H: BuildHasher`.
* Shared-reference mutation, native `get_or_insert` and `remove` through `&self`;
  configurable hasher. It exposes both `&self` and `&mut self` variants.
* Wraps `txmap::TxMap<K, V, txmap::MutexPolicy, H>`, so mutual exclusion is provided by a
  mutex policy; this is the design behind its strong concurrent results.

## 6. Recommendations

### 6.1 Implementations or benchmarks needing further investigation

1. **`clear-and-reuse` (all sizes).** The 1K groups show the worst heavy tails in the
   suite (`std_dev/median` 60–72% for `ahash`, `hashbrown`, `rustc-hash`) and the 10K
   groups still have 6–12% MAD. The benchmark mixes clear + re-insert and is likely
   sensitive to allocator/pool reuse. It should be re-run with more samples and, ideally,
   inspected for bimodality before its medians are treated as reliable.
2. **`growth` to 1M entries.** 8–15% MAD for `papaya`, `imbl`, `crossbeam-skiplist` and
   `indexmap`. At this size page-faulting and THP dominate; run with a warmed allocator
   and report a tail statistic (p95/p99), not just the median.
3. **`rustc-hash` fast-but-noisy groups.** `clear-and-reuse/10K`, `insert/u64` and the two
   single-thread `u64` throughput variants have ~8–11% MAD while `rustc-hash` is fastest.
   Its margins there are inside the noise and should not be quoted as solid wins.
4. **`indexmap`'s write-heavy `String` throughput win** (1.195 ms vs `rustc-hash`
   1.238 ms) is only a 3.6% margin in a group where the second place is a different data
   structure; confirm before citing.
5. **The `out-of-the-box` vs `same-hasher` gap.** Report the two side by side. This
   project already does, but any downstream summary that uses only out-of-the-box numbers
   will over-credit `rustc-hash` and under-credit `std`/`hashbrown`/`ahash`.

### 6.2 Measurement-quality issues that could affect conclusions

* **No tail-latency data.** Only medians (and mean) are available in
  `estimates.json`. Tail latency (p95/p99) is often the deciding factor for concurrent
  maps and cannot be assessed here.
* **Median-of-medians is not statistically tested.** "Within 10%" is a pragmatic
  threshold, not a significance test; Criterion's confidence intervals were not compared.
  Groups with sub-100 ns medians (e.g. `rpds` clone at 1.76 ns) cannot be distinguished
  at the nanosecond level.
* **`median_abs_dev` can understate heavy tails.** As shown by `clear-and-reuse/1K`,
  a low MAD can coexist with a very high `std_dev`. Both are reported here.
* **Fixed key/value types.** Only `u64` and `String` (`<16>`, `<32>`, `<128>`) keys and
  `u64` values were measured. Results may not transfer to large values, composite keys,
  or hash-collision-heavy datasets.
* **Limited thread counts and pinned threads.** Concurrent benchmarks use only 2 and 3
  threads with pinning, so behaviour beyond 3 cores and under oversubscription is
  unknown.
* **Removed implementations.** `concread`, `flurry` and `concurrent_map` are absent for
  the reasons in the README; conclusions about "the map ecosystem" should be read as
  excluding them.
* **No verification that optimisations stayed in the benchmark.** The harness uses
  `black_box` throughout, but no independent check confirms every measured operation is
  free of compiler elision. The only implausibly small result — `rpds` clone at 1.76 ns —
  is consistent with O(1) structural sharing and is therefore credible.

### 6.3 Practical takeaways

* **Choose by concurrency model first.** If the map must be shared and mutated across
  threads, `txmap` is the clear default, with `leapfrog` for `Copy` keys and `scc` /
  `papaya` as alternatives for specific read/write mixes.
* **For single-threaded code, prefer `hashbrown`, `ahash` or `std`** unless the workload
  is clearly dominated by one operation where `rustc-hash`, `indexmap`, `intmap` or
  `horde` wins decisively.
* **Persistent maps should be chosen for snapshot semantics**, not for raw speed: they
  dominate `clone` by orders of magnitude but never win write operations.
* **Do not compare data structures through their default hashers alone.** Use the
  `same-hasher` tables to judge the map implementation and the `out-of-the-box` tables to
  judge the packaged default.
