No single implementation dominates the serial groups, and the two double-digit winners are specialists. **rustc-hash** wins 22 of the 37 groups it enters (59%), but it cannot enter any same-hasher group — its wrapper has no `BenchMapNewWithHasher` — so all of its wins are out-of-the-box and its fixed `FxHash` is doing a large part of the work. **indexmap** wins 11 groups, but they are concentrated in exactly two layout-sensitive operations (all 6 `iterate` variants and 5 `growth` variants); its only non-winning appearances within 10% are the two longer-key `key-sensitivity` lookups (5–8% behind); it does not appear in the within-10% column for `lookup-hit`, `lookup-miss`, `insert`, `remove`, `clear-and-reuse` or `throughput`. **ahash** is the most consistently competitive general-purpose map (within 10% in 28 of 64 groups), and **std**/**hashbrown** are usually within ~10% of it whenever the hasher is held constant (worst case 8% on String lookups).

The persistent and ordered maps — **crossbeam-skiplist**, **rpds**, **immutable-chunkmap**, **btreemap**, **imbl** — have zero serial wins and rarely appear within 10%. That is a statement about this workload, not about quality: they solve different problems (lock-free traversal, snapshots, ordering, cheap persistent clones). **horde**, **intmap** and **rpds** each win exactly the family they specialise in: refcounted removal, integer lookups, and cloning.

#### Trade-offs that repeat across operations

- **Hashing dominates out-of-the-box rankings.** `std` goes from 1.40 µs to 227.1 ns per 100 u64 lookups when SipHash is replaced by ahash — 6.2×. Any out-of-the-box comparison that does not hold the hasher constant is partly a hasher comparison. Same-hasher groups are the correct lens for data-structure choice.
- **Dense layout buys iteration and costs point access.** indexmap iterates 100K entries in 27.85 µs but needs 1.09 µs for 100 String lookups under the common hasher, 1.30× slower than std (838.8 ns).
- **Structural sharing moves cost from clone to write.** rpds clones 100K entries in 1.4 ns, but the first 10,000 writes to that clone cost 3.62 ms, 30.9× rustc-hash. Persistent maps are cheap to snapshot and expensive to diverge.
- **Concurrency machinery taxes single-threaded throughput.** leapfrog needs 208.86 µs for 10K serial u64 read-heavy ops, 7.86× rustc-hash, yet wins 8 concurrent groups. dashmap is 1.56× behind std on same-hasher u64 get-or-insert but wins the same-hasher String version. The reverse is also true: serial champions (rustc-hash, intmap, horde, indexmap) have no `&self` mutation API and therefore do not appear in concurrent groups at all.
- **Specialisation beats generality.** `intmap` is 1.19× ahead on u64 lookups but 4.24× behind on u64 inserts; `indexmap` is 2.59× ahead on iteration but 1.30× behind on String lookups; `rpds` is ~34,000× ahead on clone but 30.9× behind on clone-then-write. None of these is a general-purpose winner.

| Implementation | Groups run | Times fastest | Times within 10% | Fastest rate | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| txmap | 48 | 37 | 44 | 77% | Broad winner; sharded transactional map with a mutex policy. |
| leapfrog | 24 | 8 | 13 | 33% | u64-only lock-free table; 8 wins, all on u64 keys. |
| scc | 48 | 1 | 3 | 2% | One win: 3-thread String write-heavy throughput. |
| dashmap | 48 | 1 | 1 | 2% | One narrow win: 3-thread String Zipfian(2.0) contention. |
| papaya | 48 | 1 | 1 | 2% | One win: 3-thread String read-only synchronization. |
| crossbeam-skiplist | 48 | 0 | 0 | 0% | No concurrent wins in this workload. |
| starshard | 48 | 0 | 0 | 0% | No concurrent wins; mid-pack. |

**txmap** is the dominant concurrent map: fastest in 37 of 48 groups and within 10% in 44 of 48. Its margins over the runner-up are 1.02–1.83×, so it is not just winning ties. **leapfrog** is the only map that beats it consistently on `u64` (8 wins, all on `u64` keys: 4 contention, 2 throughput and 2 synchronization variants; its wrapper requires `K: Copy + Eq + Hash`), **dashmap** edges the 3-thread String Zipfian(2.0) contention group by 1.01×, **papaya** wins 3-thread String read-only synchronization, and **scc** wins 3-thread String write-heavy throughput by 1.04×.

The exceptions are informative rather than random: leapfrog's lock-free `u64` table wins when the key set is small and the workload is u64 (including the 2-thread write-only single-key case); papaya's lock-free reads win when the workload is read-only on one hot String key; dashmap narrowly wins the most contended String Zipfian case; and scc's fine-grained per-bucket locking wins the 3-thread write-heavy String throughput case. None of these is a broad win. txmap's sharded transactional design is the best general compromise, especially when writes are mixed in.

Relative to the serial groups, the winner set changes completely: rustc-hash (22 serial wins) does not appear in any concurrent group because its wrapper exposes only `&mut` mutation. The same applies to intmap, horde, indexmap and the other mut-only wrappers. That is a coverage effect, not a performance result — see §5.

| Requirement | Best choice | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| General mixed read/write concurrency | **txmap** | fastest in 37/48, within 10% in 44/48 |
| `u64` keys, read-heavy or contended | **leapfrog** | 8 wins, including 247.35 µs vs txmap 558.52 µs (2.26×) |

#### Consistently near the front

- **txmap** — 44/48 concurrent groups within 10%; no catastrophic mode in the measured concurrent matrix.
- **ahash** — 28/64 serial groups within 10%; the most robust same-hasher performer alongside std/hashbrown.
- **std** and **hashbrown** — 20/64 and 24/64 within 10%; they are the reference implementation for the SwissTable family and the right baseline for any new map.
- **indexmap** — not a generalist, but unmatched within its niche: 6/6 iteration groups and 5 growth groups won.

#### Consistently behind (in these workloads)

- **crossbeam-skiplist** — 0 wins, 0 within 10% in 31 serial groups; it is a lock-free ordered map, and the benchmark rewards neither ordering nor lock-freedom in serial code.
- **btreemap** — 0 wins in 37 groups; `iterate` rewards dense layout, not key order, and comparison-based lookup is expensive at this size.
- **immutable-chunkmap** — 0 wins outside `create`/`clone`; COW writes and binary-search lookups are expensive at 10K–100K entries.
- **rpds** — 0 wins outside `clone`; 30.9× behind on clone-then-write.

### 3.3 Stability summary

Across all 112 benchmark groups, the winner is outside the 10% band (i.e. genuinely clearly ahead) in 70 groups. The remaining 42 groups are close enough that run-to-run noise or a different hasher choice could change the ranking. That is why §3.1 and §3.2 report "within 10%" counts alongside raw wins: a win by 2% is not a durable implementation advantage in a single-run data set.

The worst individual deviations are exactly where the benchmark's structure predicts them:

- **`dashmap`, clone-then-write 1K, 19.02%** — cloning a sharded map at a tiny size is dominated by per-shard lock setup and allocator state; the absolute time is 5.17 µs, so a microsecond of jitter is a large relative change.
- **`rustc-hash`, growth 100K String, out-of-the-box, 15.31%** — the largest relative deviation in the suite; the median is 3.34 ms, so a 15% swing is ~0.5 ms and is consistent with allocator/page-fault variance, not with hash-table behaviour changing.

- **`leapfrog`, synchronization 3 threads u64 write-only, 8.42%** — all threads write the same key; scheduling order and cache-line ownership dominate.
- **`std`, clear-and-reuse 10K same-hasher, 7.40%** — `clear` on a 10K map touches every bucket; the retained-capacity path interacts with allocator state.

**Robustness conclusion.** For the large, non-concurrent operations (insert, lookup, remove, iterate) the medians are stable enough (MAD < 2% for most) that differences above ~10% are likely real. For `growth` above 100K entries, sub-10% differences are not resolved. For `clone` at nanosecond scale, only the order-of-magnitude conclusion is meaningful.

One statistical caveat deserves emphasis: in `txmap`'s 3-thread u64 balanced throughput group the CV is 41.9% (mean 397.6 µs, SD 166.6 µs) while the relative MAD is only 0.55% (median 379.1 µs). The mean is pulled by at least one slow iteration, but the median is stable. This is why the report uses medians, and why "mean" numbers in a concurrent Criterion report should not be compared without checking MAD.

### 5.2 What each gap removes from the comparison

- **No `BenchMapNewWithHasher`** — `btreemap`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `immutable-chunkmap`, `intmap`, `rustc-hash` — removes the implementation from every `same-hasher` variant. Same-hasher serial groups therefore contain 11–14 implementations versus 15–19 for out-of-the-box serial groups. This is the main reason same-hasher and out-of-the-box tables cannot be compared by row count.
- **No `BenchMapClone`** — `crossbeam-skiplist`, `leapfrog` — removes them from `clone` and `clone-then-write` (17 implementations instead of 19).
- **No `BenchMapMutClear`** — `rpds`, `immutable-chunkmap`, `leapfrog` — removes them from `clear-and-reuse` (16 implementations).
- **No get-or-insert at all** — `horde`, `rpds` — removes them from `get-or-insert` (15–17 implementations out-of-the-box, 11–12 same-hasher). The other mut-only maps implement `BenchMapMutGetOrInsert` and do appear. The excluded `concread`, `concurrent-map` and `flurry` wrappers implement neither trait.
- **No shared-ref writes** — all mut-only wrappers (ahash, btreemap, hashbrown, hashlink, horde, imbl, immutable-chunkmap, indexmap, intmap, rpds, rustc-hash, std) — removes them from every concurrent group. `contention`, `synchronization` and concurrent `throughput` contain only the seven maps with shared-ref writes — crossbeam-skiplist, dashmap, leapfrog, papaya, scc, starshard and txmap — and leapfrog appears only in `u64` groups, so each group has 6–7 implementations.

### 5.3 Trait bounds that restrict the matrix

- **`leapfrog`** requires `K: Eq + Hash + Copy` and `V: leapfrog::Value` (also `Copy`). It can therefore never be benchmarked with `String` keys, so its wins on `u64` (up to 2.26×) do not generalise to string-keyed workloads. Its wrapper also has no `Clone` or `clear`.
- **`intmap`** requires `K: IntKey`, i.e. integer keys only. Its strong `u64` lookup/removal results say nothing about string-keyed maps, and it is absent from every `String<32>` group.
- **`starshard`** requires `K: Clone + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync` and `H: BuildHasher + Clone + Send + Sync`, and uses `DEFAULT_SHARDS`; the benchmark does not tune the shard count, which is a first-order parameter for concurrent maps.
- **`crossbeam-skiplist`** requires `K: Ord` (plus `Send`), and its wrapper has no `Clone`. It is an ordered structure competing in unordered-map workloads.
- **`txmap`** is instantiated as `TxMap<K, V, txmap::MutexPolicy, H>` — a mutex-based policy, not a lock-free one. Its dominant concurrent results therefore describe this specific policy; other `txmap` policies are not measured.
- **`papaya`** performs every operation through `pin()`, i.e. it acquires an epoch guard per call. A caller that pins once and performs many operations would see different per-operation costs.
- **`scc`** uses `clear_sync` for `clear` and `RandomState` as its default hasher; `papaya` uses `pin().clear()`. Both are valid clear implementations, but "clear" is not the same operation across all crates (see §5.4).

### 5.4 Semantic and measurement caveats

- **`indexmap` removal uses `swap_remove`**, which moves the last entry into the removed slot and changes iteration order. Its `remove` result is therefore not order-preserving; a `shift_remove` would be O(n) and is not measured. This is a semantic difference from maps whose iteration order is unspecified, not just a performance difference.
- **`horde` clear is `replace(std::iter::empty(), 0)`**, which builds a new empty table with capacity 0 and swaps it in. Unlike `clear()` on std/hashbrown, it does not retain the old allocation, so horde's `clear-and-reuse` result includes reallocation on refill and is not a like-for-like capacity-retention measurement.
- **`clone` includes teardown of the clone.** Each iteration runs `map.clone_map()` and immediately drops the temporary, so the timed region includes the destructor. For deep-copy maps the drop walks every entry; for persistent maps it is a refcount decrement. The comparison is "clone and discard", not "clone and keep".
- **`create` measures construction plus destruction** of an empty map (`black_box(Map::new())` as a temporary). `immutable-chunkmap`'s 2.73 µs per 10,000 maps (0.27 ns/map) is below one CPU cycle per map and is best read as "the optimizer removed the work", not as a real 2× win over `rustc-hash` (5.46 µs).
- **`get-or-insert` is 50/50, not 90/10.** `GET_OR_INSERT_HIT_RATIO = 0.90` exists in `src/config.rs` but is not referenced; the benchmark issues 10,000 existing-key calls and 10,000 missing-key calls.
- **`clear-and-reuse` uses flat sampling** (`SLOW_SAMPLING_MODE = Flat`), which is appropriate for long-running groups but produces a single flat sample set rather than the linear sampling used elsewhere. Its median relative MAD is ~7% at 10K entries for std (see §4).
- **Concurrent groups measure wall-clock time for all threads**, not per-operation latency or scaling. A 3-thread run performs 1.5× the work of a 2-thread run; throughput comparisons are only valid at the same thread count. There is no 1-thread concurrent baseline and no memory-bandwidth or CPU-utilisation measurement.
- **`iterate` sums values but does not verify key order or content.** It rewards contiguous layout and prefetching, not ordered traversal. `btreemap`'s ordered iteration is not measured as an ordered traversal.
- **Same-hasher still uses `ahash` for every map**, which favours maps whose internal design matches ahash's short, high-quality hashes (the SwissTable family). It removes hasher *choice* but not hasher *interaction* with probing or layout.
- **No memory measurement** is present: allocation count, peak RSS, and per-entry overhead are not captured. A map that is 2× slower but uses half the memory would look strictly worse here.

### 5.5 Per-implementation wrapper notes

- **`ahash`** — `ahash::AHashMap<K,V,H>`; lookups/inserts need `K: Hash + Eq` and `V: Clone`; clone additionally needs `K: Clone` and `H: Clone`. Has `BenchMapNewWithHasher`, but mutation is `&mut`-only, so it never enters a concurrent group.
- **`btreemap`** — `std::collections::BTreeMap`; `K: Ord` replaces hashing entirely, and `V: Clone`. No hasher variant, no `&self` writes.
- **`crossbeam-skiplist`** — `crossbeam_skiplist::SkipMap`; `K: Ord` for reads and `K: Ord + Send + 'static` for writes. No `NewWithHasher`, no `Clone`; it does expose `&self` insert/get-or-insert/remove and ordered iteration.
- **`dashmap`** — `dashmap::DashMap`; `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; the hasher variant additionally needs `H: Clone`. `&self` writes through per-shard locks.
- **`hashbrown`** — `hashbrown::HashMap` with `DefaultHashBuilder` (foldhash in the pinned version); `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; has the hasher variant, no `&self` writes.
- **`hashlink`** — `hashlink::LinkedHashMap`; `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`. Its `NewWithHasher` adds no extra bounds; mutation is `&mut`-only.
- **`horde`** — `horde::SyncTable`; construction needs `H: Default`, clone/insert/remove need `K: Clone`, and `clear` needs only `K: Hash`. It has no get-or-insert implementation, so it is absent from that group; `clear` is `replace(empty, 0)`, which does not retain capacity.
- **`imbl`** — `GenericHashMap<K,V,H,DefaultSharedPtr>` with `RandomState` default; `H: Default`; `&mut`-only writes.
- **`immutable-chunkmap`** — `immutable_chunkmap::map::MapM`; `K: Clone + Ord`, `V: Clone`. No hasher variant, no `clear`, no `&self` writes; every write is copy-on-write.
- **`indexmap`** — `indexmap::IndexMap`; `K: Hash + Eq`, clone needs only `K: Clone`. Has the hasher variant and `clear`; `remove` is `swap_remove`, not `shift_remove`.
- **`intmap`** — `intmap::IntMap`; `K: IntKey` (integers only) and `get` takes the key by value (`get(*key)`). No hasher variant, no `String` keys, `&mut`-only writes.
- **`leapfrog`** — `leapfrog::LeapMap`; `K: Eq + Hash + Copy`, `V: leapfrog::Value` (effectively `Copy`). Has `NewWithHasher` and `&self` writes, but no `Clone` and no `clear`, and it can never run `String` keys.
- **`papaya`** — `papaya::HashMap` with `RandomState` default; `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; clone needs `K: Clone`. Every operation goes through `pin()`, and `clear` is `pin().clear()`; writes are `&self`.
- **`rpds-hash-trie-map`** — `rpds::HashTrieMap<K,V,RcK,H>`; `K: Eq + Hash`, `H: BuildHasher + Clone + Default`. No get-or-insert, no `clear`, no `&self` writes; `RcK` makes it `!Send`.
- **`rustc-hash`** — `rustc_hash::FxHashMap`; `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`. The hasher is fixed (`FxBuildHasher`), there is no `NewWithHasher`, and writes are `&mut`-only.
- **`scc`** — `scc::HashMap` with `RandomState` default; `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`. `clear` uses `clear_sync`; writes are `&self` and rely on scc's `sdd` deferred-destruction scheme.
- **`starshard`** — `starshard::ShardedHashMap` with `DEFAULT_SHARDS` and `rustc_hash::FxBuildHasher` by default; `K: Clone + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync`, `H: BuildHasher + Clone + Send + Sync`. Writes are `&self`; shard count is not tuned.
- **`std`** — `std::collections::HashMap` with `RandomState` (SipHash-1-3); `K: Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`; clone needs only `K: Clone`. Has the hasher variant, no `&self` writes.
- **`txmap`** — `txmap::TxMap<K,V,txmap::MutexPolicy,H>`; `K: Hash + Eq` for reads, `K: Clone + Hash + Eq` for get-or-insert. The mutex policy is hard-coded in the wrapper; writes are `&self`.
- **`concread`** (excluded) — `K: Clone + Debug + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync + 'static`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync + 'static`. No `Clone`, no hasher variant, no get-or-insert; every benchmark marks it too slow.
- **`concurrent-map`** (excluded) — `K: Minimum + Clone + Send + Sync + 'static`. The underlying map is not `Sync`, so the wrapper keeps a mutex-protected master plus per-thread clones; no get-or-insert; marked too slow.
- **`flurry`** (excluded) — `K: Ord + Hash + Send + Sync`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync`; the constructor seeds a `seize::Collector` per map. Has `NewWithHasher` and `Clone` but no get-or-insert; marked too slow.

### 5.6 Excluded implementations (recap)

| Implementation | Why it has no usable data |
|---|---|
| `concread` | Every benchmark marks it "too slow"; it also lacks `Clone` and a configurable hasher. |
| `concurrent-map` | Every benchmark marks it "too slow"; the underlying map is `Send` but not `Sync`, so the wrapper shares it via a mutex-protected master plus thread-local clones. |

## 6. Recommendations

The rankings below are the practical summary of §2–§5. They apply to this benchmark suite (10K-entry working sets, 100-operation batches, 2–3 threads, one run) and should be re-validated against the target workload before adoption.

| Decision | Recommendation | Why (evidence) | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default single-threaded map | **std** (or `hashbrown`/`ahash`) | Same-hasher u64 lookups and String inserts are within ~1%; String lookups differ by up to 8%; all three are stable and well understood | std's default SipHash costs it 2.6–6.7× on lookups and 2.0–2.4× on inserts relative to ahash; choose the hasher deliberately |
| Read-mostly, `u64` keys | **intmap** for pure lookups; **rustc-hash** for mixed reads/writes | intmap wins lookup-hit/lookup-miss by 1.19–1.44×; rustc-hash wins throughput and growth | intmap is 4.24× slower on inserts and cannot use string keys |
| Read-mostly, `String` keys | **rustc-hash** (default hasher) or **std/ahash** (fixed ahash) | rustc-hash wins out-of-the-box String lookups by 1.28× and both out-of-the-box insert variants; std/ahash/hashbrown tie under the common hasher | rustc-hash cannot enter same-hasher groups, so it is absent from the like-for-like comparison |
| Iteration-heavy | **indexmap** | Wins all 6 iterate variants; 2.59× ahead at 100K entries | Costs ~30% on String point lookups and does not win any lookup group |
| Removal-heavy | **horde** | Wins 3 of 4 remove variants; 1.69× ahead on String same-hasher | Wrapper is `&mut`-only, so it cannot be used in concurrent code; clear does not retain capacity |
| Clone-heavy, read-mostly clones | **rpds-hash-trie-map** | 1.4 ns per clone at every size; ~34,000× faster than a deep copy at 100K | The first writes to a clone are 30.9× slower than rustc-hash; no `clear`, no get-or-insert |
| Clone-heavy, then mutate | **rustc-hash** (or std/ahash) | 117.05 µs for clone + 10K inserts at 100K vs 1.97–3.86 ms for persistent maps | Deep copy is O(n) in both time and memory |
| Concurrent mixed read/write | **txmap** | Fastest in 37/48 concurrent groups, within 10% in 44/48 | Hard-coded `MutexPolicy`; no lock-free policy is measured; its `u64` margins are 1.02–1.36× and it is more than 10% behind in three 3-thread `u64` cases |
| Concurrent `u64`, read-heavy | **leapfrog** | 8 wins, including 2.26× over txmap in 3-thread Zipfian(2.0) contention | `K: Copy` only — no String keys; no Clone; no clear |
| Concurrent read-only hot String key | **papaya** | Wins 3-thread String read-only by 1.10× over txmap | Only wins the read-only extreme; mid-pack when writes are present |
| Concurrent write-heavy String, 3 threads | **scc** (marginal) | Fastest in that one variant, 1.04× ahead of txmap | Wins 1 of 48 groups; not a general txmap replacement |
| Hot-loop map construction | **std**/**imbl** (lazy allocation) or **immutable-chunkmap** | 13.63 µs per 10K empty maps vs 1.68–10.23 ms for sharded maps | immutable-chunkmap's 2.73 µs is likely optimised away; do not rely on it |
| Ordered iteration / range queries | **btreemap** (not benchmarked for ordering) | No speed win here, but the only ordered comparison-based map in the set | 0 wins; do not choose it for point throughput |
| Lock-free ordered traversal | **crossbeam-skiplist** | No speed win here, but lock-free iteration is its purpose | 0 wins and 0 within-10% in 31 serial groups |

### 6.1 What to do with these results

1. **Pick the family first, then the hasher.** The same `std` map moves by 6.2× on u64 lookups when SipHash is replaced by ahash. Hasher choice is at least as important as map choice for out-of-the-box performance.
2. **Use the same-hasher tables for data-structure decisions.** The out-of-the-box tables answer "which map + hasher should I use as-is"; the same-hasher tables answer "which data structure is faster".
3. **Treat sub-10% wins as ties.** On this single run, 42 of 112 groups have the runner-up within 10% of the winner, and 18 of those are within 2%. Choose on API, memory, and semantics when the gap is small.
4. **Re-run before committing.** The data set has no run-to-run confidence interval. Growth groups above 100K entries, clone groups at nanosecond scale, and concurrent groups with high scheduling variance should be re-measured at least 3–5 times, and ideally with the real key type, hasher, thread count, and map size.
5. **Measure what this suite does not.** Memory footprint, tail latency (p99 rather than median), 8–16 thread scaling, and per-map tuning (shard counts, `txmap` policy, hasher seeds) are all outside the current data. A map that wins the median can still lose on p99 or memory.

### 6.2 Bottom line

- For **single-threaded, general-purpose** use, the SwissTable family (`std`, `hashbrown`, `ahash`) is the safe default, and the choice between them is mostly a hasher/API decision, not a speed decision.
- For **specialised single-threaded** use, `intmap` (integer lookups), `rustc-hash` (out-of-the-box mixed workloads), `indexmap` (iteration), `horde` (removal) and `rpds` (clone) each win their niche by 1.2× to ~34,000× and lose elsewhere.
- For **concurrent** use, `txmap` is the broad winner, with `leapfrog` (u64, read-heavy), `papaya` (read-only String) and `scc` (write-heavy String) as targeted alternatives.
- For **ordered, persistent, or lock-free-traversal** requirements, choose `btreemap`, `imbl`/`rpds`/`immutable-chunkmap`, or `crossbeam-skiplist` for semantic reasons — this benchmark provides no throughput justification for them.
