### 3.3 Trade-offs

There are clear specialization patterns — no single implementation wins across the board:

- **leapfrog** is the concurrent champion but requires `K: Copy` + `V: Value` (excluded from String-key and clone/clear benchmarks), is not fastest at 100 000/4-thread get-or-insert (collapses to 6.55 ms vs scc's 1.34 ms), and has no `clear`/`Clone` API.
- **indexmap** wins iteration by ~2.5× but is ~1.7–6× slower than rustc-hash at insert/remove/lookup.
- **rpds-hash-trie-map** wins clone by an order of magnitude (O(1)) but is an order of magnitude or more slower at mutation (8× on pure insert, up to ~270× on mixed serial workloads), because every write copies path nodes.
- **dashmap** beats leapfrog on large write-heavy Zipfian workloads but loses to it on read-heavy ones.
- **txmap** wins the 1 000-entry concurrent groups but falls behind at 10 000/100 000 entries.
- **horde** wins same-hasher insert and remove (out-of-the-box it is ~2–2.7× slower than rustc-hash), suggesting its default hasher is comparatively weak while its table itself is fast.
- **rustc-hash**'s serial dominance shrinks under a common hasher: it has no custom-hasher support and is simply benefiting from the fast Fx hash.

### 3.4 Consistently close implementations

- **hashbrown**: within 10 % of the fastest in 26 of 124 groups (21 %) — the most consistently competitive map; never more than ~1.2× behind in serial ops.
- **std**: within 10 % in 17 groups (all same-hasher groups).
- **ahash**: within 10 % in 22 groups.
- **hashlink**: within 10 % in 11 groups.
- **scc**: within 10 % in 8 groups — the only concurrent map that regularly lands within 10 % of leapfrog at higher thread counts.

## 4. Stability

Metrics: `median_abs_dev` (MAD) and `std_dev` from `estimates.json`, expressed relative to the median. Thresholds for "high variance": relative MAD > 5 % or relative std_dev > 10 %.

### 4.1 Overall picture

- **Serial benchmarks are extremely stable**: nearly all serial groups have relative MAD < 2 % (most < 1 %). Exceptions: `create` for indexmap (MAD 19.3 % — construction cost jitter), `remove same-hasher 1 000` for hashlink (MAD 28.2 %), `lookup-hit 100 000` for hashlink (MAD 5.1 %), `iterate 10 000` for ahash/hashbrown/std/horde (MAD 5–10 %).
- **Concurrent 4-thread benchmarks are noisy**: the worst cases have std_dev of 20–100 % of the median, driven by scheduler/contention variance. This is expected for short (2 s) measurement windows on a multi-threaded workload.

### 4.2 Fast implementations with unusually noisy measurements

These are cases where the *winner* (or a close contender) itself has high variance, so the win is less certain:

| Group | Fastest | Median | rel. MAD | rel. std_dev |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| workload balanced 1 000 / 4 th | leapfrog | 562 µs | 8.7 % | 104.3 % |
| workload read-heavy 100 000 / 4 th | leapfrog | 981 µs | 63.8 % | 72.4 % |
| workload write-heavy 100 000 / 4 th | leapfrog | 840 µs | 2.1 % | 61.7 % |
| get-or-insert 10 000 / 4 th | leapfrog | 1.07 ms | 19.2 % | 17.1 % |
| get-or-insert 1 000 / 4 th | leapfrog | 523 µs | 8.3 % | 20.0 % |
| workload balanced 10 000 / 4 th | leapfrog | 1.12 ms | 15.9 % | 17.4 % (scc within 1 %) |
| zipfian read-heavy 100 000 / 4 th | scc | 1.01 ms | 3.7 % | 28.1 % |

leapfrog is the fastest in most concurrent groups, but several of its 4-thread measurements have relative std_dev of 17–104 %, and one (read-heavy 100 000/4 th) has MAD 63.8 %. Its margins over the field are nevertheless usually large (1.4–2×), so the ranking is likely correct, but the *magnitude* of the advantage at 4 threads should be re-measured.

Other noisy rows (slower implementations, so conclusions are unaffected, but flagged for measurement quality):
- rpds-hash-trie-map in serial workloads: `workload write-heavy 100 000/1 th` rel. MAD 59.0 %; `workload read-heavy 100 000/1 th` rel. MAD 40.6 %; `workload balanced 100 000/1 th` rel. std 28.2 %.
- papaya: `zipfian balanced 100 000/4 th` rel. MAD 50.9 %; `zipfian read-heavy 100 000/4 th` rel. MAD 50.9 %.
- dashmap in 4-thread groups: `zipfian read-heavy 100 000/4 th` rel. MAD 30.1 %; `get-or-insert 100 000/4 th` rel. MAD 24.8 %; `workload balanced 100 000/4 th` rel. MAD 27.7 % (dashmap was within 10 % of fastest in several of these).
- starshard in 100 000/4-thread groups: rel. MAD 15–21 %.

The pattern is consistent with **thread-count-4 groups having the poorest measurement quality**, not with any specific map being inherently unstable.

## 5. Implementation Limitations (from `src/maps/` wrappers)

Per-implementation findings based on the trait implementations in the wrapper code:

- **ahash** (`AhashBenchMap`): requires `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone` for get/get_or_insert. Supports custom hashers (`BenchMapNewWithHasher`, `H: BuildHasher`). No unusual requirements.
- **std** (`StdBenchMap`): requires `K: Hash + Eq` (get/insert/remove), `V: Clone` for get/get_or_insert. Supports custom hashers. No unusual requirements. Note its `BenchMapNew` has no `K`/`V` bounds (empty map works for any type).
- **hashbrown** (`HashbrownBenchMap`): same shape as std; supports custom hashers. `K: Clone`, `V: Clone`, `H: BuildHasher + Clone` needed for clone. No unusual requirements.
- **rustc-hash** (`RustCHashBenchMap`): **no custom-hasher support** (fixed Fx hasher — no `BenchMapNewWithHasher`), so it appears only in out-of-the-box groups. Requires `K: Hash + Eq`; `V: Clone` for get/get_or_insert. Fx hashing is fast but not strong against adversarial key distributions (unchecked in these benchmarks).
- **hashlink** (`HashlinkBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`; maintains insertion order (LinkedHashMap) with extra link bookkeeping; supports custom hashers. `BenchMapNew` requires only `H: Default`.
- **indexmap** (`IndexMapBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`; supports custom hashers. Removal uses **`swap_remove`** (order-unstable removal — could surprise callers relying on insertion order after deletes). Iteration is contiguous-vector backed (source of its iterate wins).
- **btreemap** (`BTreeMapBenchMap`): `K: Ord`; **no hasher at all** (no `BenchMapNewWithHasher`); sorted iteration; O(log n) complexity. Excluded from same-hasher groups.
- **immutable-chunkmap** (`ImmutableChunkMapBenchMap`): `K: Clone + Ord`, `V: Clone`. Persistent/copy-on-write (`MapM`); **no custom-hasher support**; **no `clear`** (excluded from clear-and-reuse); no shared-ref mutation (mutation returns new map via COW), so excluded from concurrent groups.
- **imbl** (`ImblBenchMap`): persistent `GenericHashMap`; mutation requires `K: Clone + Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone`, `H: BuildHasher + Clone`. Supports custom hashers. Excluded from concurrent groups (mutation needs `&mut`).
- **rpds** (`RpdsHashTrieMapBenchMap`): persistent `HashTrieMap` with **archery `RcK` pointer — not `Send`/`Sync`**, so excluded from all concurrent groups. No entry API: get-or-insert is emulated as get + `insert_mut` (two lookups). **No `clear`** (excluded from clear-and-reuse). Clone is O(1) shared-structure. `V: Clone` needed for get/remove paths.
- **concurrent-map** (`ConcurrentMapBenchMap`): unusual bounds `K: 'static + Clone + Minimum + Send + Sync` (the `Minimum` bound requires keys to have a minimum element — works for `u64`, would not work for arbitrary types); **`Send` but not `Sync`** — a `&ConcurrentMap` cannot be shared across threads, so it is excluded from all concurrent benches despite being a concurrent data structure. No custom hasher; **no `clear`**.
- **crossbeam-skiplist** (`CrossbeamSkiplistBenchMap`): `K: Ord` (+ `Send + 'static` for mutation), `V: Send + 'static`. **No `Clone`** (excluded from clone/clone-then-write). **No custom hasher** (excluded from same-hasher groups). Sorted skiplist.
- **dashmap** (`DashMapBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`, `H: BuildHasher + Clone`; supports custom hashers; concurrent (sharded, `&self` mutation). No unusual requirements; full API surface (clone, clear, entry, remove).
- **flurry** (`FlurryBenchMap`): wrapper exists but every bench call site is commented out ("too slow", and construction creates a `seize::Collector` per map). Notable bounds: `K: Hash + Ord + Sync + Send + Clone`, `V: Sync + Send + Clone` for mutation. No data.
- **horde** (`HordeBenchMap`): `K: Clone + Hash + Eq`, `V: Clone` for mutation (values are cloned in/out); supports custom hashers; concurrent (`SyncTable`, `&self` read + `&mut`-style write methods — note the wrapper mutates through `&mut self` only, so horde is excluded from shared-ref concurrent benches).
- **leapfrog** (`LeapfrogBenchMap`): **`K: Eq + Hash + Copy`** (keys must be `Copy`) and `V: leapfrog::Value`; excluded from String-key benches (key-sensitivity String groups). **No `Clone`** (excluded from clone/clone-then-write); **no `clear`** (excluded from clear-and-reuse). Supports custom hashers; concurrent through `&self` (`try_insert`, `insert`, `remove`). `Value` bound adds restrictions beyond `Clone`.
- **papaya** (`PapayaBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`, `H: BuildHasher`; concurrent; every operation internally takes a `pin()` guard (per-call pin cost). Supports custom hashers; full API surface (clone, clear, entry, remove).
- **scc** (`SccBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq`, `H: BuildHasher`; concurrent; wrappers use the `*_sync` API variants. Supports custom hashers; full API surface.
- **starshard** (`StarshardBenchMap`): `K: Clone + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync`, `H: BuildHasher + Clone + Send + Sync`; default hasher is **`rustc_hash::FxBuildHasher`**; wrapper hard-codes **8 shards** (`with_shards_and_hasher(8, …)`) — shard-count sensitivity is unexplored. Concurrent; supports custom hashers; full API surface.
- **txmap** (`TxMapBenchMap`): `K: Hash + Eq` for insert/remove; `K: Clone` needed for clone and get-or-insert; transactional map with `MutexPolicy`; supports custom hashers; concurrent; full API surface.
- **concread** (`ConcreadBenchMap`): wrapper exists but is commented out of all benches ("too slow"). Bounds: `K: Clone + Debug + Hash + Eq + Send + Sync + 'static`, `V: Clone + Send + Sync + 'static`. No custom hasher; **no `Clone`**. No data.

## 6. Recommendations

### Further investigation

1. **leapfrog at 100 000 entries / 4 threads (get-or-insert)** — leapfrog collapses from ~670 µs (3 threads) to 6.55 ms (4 threads) while scc/papaya stay ~1.3–1.4 ms. This is a dramatic regression vs. its otherwise dominant concurrent performance and should be investigated (resize contention? table-level lock?).
2. **4-thread concurrent results generally** — the highest-variance measurements (rel. std_dev up to 104 %) are concentrated at 4 threads. Re-run the concurrent suites with longer measurement times (the current 2 s window at 4 threads is short) and/or pinning verification before treating 4-thread margins as definitive.
3. **leapfrog's Copy-key restriction** — it cannot be used with `String`/heap keys at all. Its concurrent dominance applies only to `Copy` key domains; consider a follow-up benchmark of other concurrent maps with string keys.
4. **horde under a common hasher** — horde wins same-hasher insert/remove but is mid-pack out-of-the-box, implying its default hasher is a bottleneck. If its custom-hasher path is representative, it may be under-rated in the out-of-the-box results.
5. **rustc-hash's serial wins** — all serial out-of-the-box wins are driven partly by the weak-but-fast Fx hasher. For production guidance, weight these against hash-quality/DoS requirements (untrusted input).
6. **rpds/imbl/immutable-chunkmap** — high MAD (up to 59 %) in serial workload benches, likely allocator/GC effects of persistent structures; re-measure with longer windows if persistent-map conclusions are important.

### Measurement-quality issues affecting conclusions

- **No baseline data** — every conclusion is relative to a single run of absolute medians; no change-detection or across-machine variance data exists.
- **Short measurement window (2 s) for 4-thread concurrent groups** — many std_dev values exceed 20–100 % of the median; rankings at 4 threads are directionally reliable only where margins are large (most leapfrog wins are 1.4–2×).
- **`clone` benchmark floor effect** — rpds's ~1.1–1.2 ns clone is at the timer-resolution noise floor; the only meaningful statement is "O(1)-ish vs. O(n)".
- **Fixed shard count (8) for starshard** — results are specific to this configuration.
- **warm-up 1 s** may be short for the larger concurrent workloads (100 000 entries), contributing to the observed bimodal/noisy 4-thread distributions.
