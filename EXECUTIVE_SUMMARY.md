# Executive Summary: Rust Map Benchmark Results

We analysed the Criterion.rs results in `target/criterion/` covering **20 map implementations**, **112 benchmark groups** and **1,349 individual measurements** across construction, growth, insert, get-or-insert, lookup (hit/miss), remove, clear-and-reuse, iteration, cloning, key sensitivity, and serial/concurrent mixed workloads. All figures are Criterion median point estimates; lower is better. The `base` and `new` estimates are identical (no change delta), so this is an absolute single-run comparison, not a regression report.

The results split cleanly along a concurrency boundary. For **single-threaded work the decisive map is `rustc-hash` (`FxHashMap`)**, which was fastest in 26 of the 37 serial groups it entered and within 10% of the winner in 28 of them. Its advantage comes largely from the `FxHasher`: when every map is given the same hasher, the ranking collapses onto the SwissTable family — `ahash`, `hashbrown` and `std` finish within a few percent of one another for insert, lookup, get-or-insert and small-key growth. For **concurrent work the decisive map is `txmap` (with `MutexPolicy`)**, which won 38 of 48 concurrent groups, including 17 of 24 single-hot-key synchronization cases and 11 of 12 mixed-throughput cases. `leapfrog` is the main lock-free alternative and is actually faster for the most skewed `u64` contention patterns.

Several maps own a specific niche rather than the general case. **`indexmap` is the only sensible choice for iteration** — it is ~3–4× faster than the general hash maps because entries live in a contiguous vector — and it also wins `String` growth under a shared hasher. **`intmap` dominates `u64` lookups and removals out of the box** by avoiding hashing entirely, but is integer-only. **Persistent/structurally-shared maps (`rpds`, `imbl`, `immutable-chunkmap`) clone in nanoseconds** versus tens of microseconds to milliseconds for every mutable map — a category-defining difference, not a small edge. **`horde` unexpectedly wins removal** under a shared hasher. At the other end, `starshard`, `btreemap`, `imbl`, `crossbeam-skiplist` and `concurrent-map` never placed within 10% of the winner in any group they entered.

The data also exposes design trade-offs worth designing around. Concurrent and sharded maps (`txmap`, `dashmap`, `papaya`, `starshard`, `concurrent-map`) cost roughly **0.2–0.9 µs to construct an empty map**, versus essentially zero for `rustc-hash` and `immutable-chunkmap`; they are poor choices for short-lived maps. `indexmap`'s fast iteration is paid for with slower random insertion. Persistent maps amortise snapshots but pay heavily on every mutation. Under real contention, `txmap` and `leapfrog` win while fixed 8-shard (`starshard`) and per-thread-clone (`concurrent-map`) designs fare worst, and key skew (Zipfian) hurts `txmap` more than `leapfrog`.

The measurements are stable enough to act on. The median relative MAD is ~0.4–1.4%, and the winning result in every group has relative MAD below 13%; no winner is a noise artefact. The noisiest cases are large `1M` growth runs and `100K` iteration/clear benchmarks, where allocation and allocator state dominate. Two caveats temper the headline: (1) **`txmap`'s dominance should be independently verified** — its policy configuration and shared-map semantics are the single most consequential result and deserve a targeted audit before being treated as a general recommendation; and (2) the **`concurrent-map` wrapper uses a thread-local clone workaround because the underlying type is `Send` but not `Sync`**, so its slow numbers reflect the wrapper rather than necessarily the crate used idiomatically.

**Bottom line for engineering decisions:** use `rustc-hash` (or `ahash`/`hashbrown`/`std` if you need a configurable or DoS-resistant hasher) for single-threaded maps; use `txmap` for heavy concurrent read/write mixes, with `leapfrog` as the lock-free fallback for skewed `u64` write-heavy loads; use `indexmap` when iteration order or traversal speed matters; use `rpds`/`imbl`/`immutable-chunkmap` when cheap snapshots or persistence are required; and treat out-of-the-box results as the "drop-in" answer and same-hasher results as the "which data structure is actually faster" answer.

**Suggested follow-ups:** re-run and audit the `txmap` and `concurrent-map` concurrency results; separate O(1)-clone persistent maps from O(n) mutable maps in future reporting; extend same-hasher and String-key coverage beyond the SwissTable family; and add repeated runs/baselines for the handful of allocation-bound groups with >10% relative MAD.
