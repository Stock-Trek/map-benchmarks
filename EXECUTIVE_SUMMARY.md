# Executive Summary — Map Implementation Benchmark Report

**Date:** 2026-08-21 · **Source:** Criterion.rs reports in `target/criterion/` (58 benchmark groups, 19 map implementations, median timings)

## Headline finding

**There is no single best map.** Performance splits cleanly along four architectural lines: (1) single-threaded hash maps, (2) concurrent maps optimized for hot-key contention, (3) concurrent maps optimized for multi-threaded mixed throughput, and (4) persistent (structurally-shared) maps. Any engineering decision should first pin down which of these regimes the workload lives in, then pick the corresponding winner.

## The four leaders

- **`rustc-hash`** is the single-threaded champion: fastest in 17 of 58 operations and within 10% in 19 — covering bulk insert, growth, lookup hits, remove, get-or-insert, clear-and-reuse, and all 1-thread mixed throughput. Caveats: it uses FxHash (fast but not DoS-hardened), supports no custom hasher, and iterates poorly (~3× slower than indexmap). It competes only against other maps' *default* hashers.
- **`leapfrog`** is the concurrency-stress king: fastest in all 9 contention/synchronization groups (3 threads hammering hot keys, uniform through Zipfian(2.0)) plus 3-thread read-heavy throughput, typically by 1.3–3.8×. It requires `Copy` keys and `leapfrog::Value` values, has no `clear()`, and is mediocre serially (e.g. ~5.7× slower than rustc-hash on insert).
- **`txmap`** is the best scaling concurrent map for realistic mixed workloads: fastest in all three 2-thread and two of three 3-thread throughput groups (balanced and write-heavy). It is not the best under single-key contention.
- **`indexmap`** is unmatched for iteration (2.5–3× faster than any hash map at every size) thanks to dense entry storage — the obvious choice when full-map scans matter, despite mid-pack insert/lookup.

When the hasher is equalized (all maps given the same `ahash::RandomState`), the field tightens dramatically: **`ahash`** becomes the most consistent winner (7 of 18 same-hasher groups), with `horde` (which is held back by a slow default hasher), `hashbrown`, and `std` all effectively equivalent. For single-threaded hash-map needs, `ahash`/`hashbrown`/`std` with a shared hasher are interchangeable within ~5–10% — the report treats sub-10% gaps as practically equivalent.

## Notable trade-offs and surprises

- **Hasher choice dominates structure in some cases.** `std` goes from 937ns to 253ns on lookup-hit simply by swapping SipHash for ahash; `horde`'s insert drops from 326µs to 140µs. Benchmarks that only look at default-hasher configs (including rustc-hash's wins) overstate structural differences.
- **Persistent maps are O(1)-ish to clone but pay heavily to mutate.** `rpds` clones a 100k-entry map in ~1.2ns (a pointer bump) yet inserts at 1.45ms vs 149µs for rustc-hash. Use them only where snapshotting/copy semantics dominate.
- **`txmap` and `leapfrog` pay for their concurrency when used single-threaded.** `txmap` is ~6.4× slower than rustc-hash on 1-thread throughput and 1.3–4.8× slower than leapfrog under hot-key contention; `leapfrog` is ~5.7× slower than rustc-hash on insert.
- **`concurrent-map`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `starshard`, `papaya`, `dashmap`, `imbl`, `scc`, and `btreemap` never won a single group.** `concurrent-map` isn't even `Sync` (excluded from multi-threaded runs); `starshard` is hard-coded to 8 shards in the harness and was consistently slowest of the concurrent maps.
- **Measurement scope is limited.** Concurrency was exercised at only 2–3 threads, so scaling conclusions beyond that are speculative. The `create` and small `clone-then-write` groups are noisy (relative std up to 35% / MAD up to 27%), and the sub-nanosecond persistent-clone timings sit near the measurement floor. `concread` and `flurry` were excluded from all benchmarks as "too slow" and have no data.

## Recommendations for engineering leadership

1. **Default single-threaded choice:** `rustc-hash` if keys are small, hot, and not attacker-controlled; otherwise `hashbrown`/`ahash` with an ahash hasher (the safest, most consistent pick — within 10% of fastest in 16 of 43 groups).
2. **Default concurrent choice:** `txmap` for balanced multi-threaded workloads; switch to `leapfrog` when workloads contend on hot keys or scale to more readers. Validate at higher thread counts before committing.
3. **Order/iteration needs:** `indexmap` for insertion-ordered iteration; `btreemap` only when `Ord`-ordered traversal is required.
4. **Snapshot semantics:** `rpds`/`imbl` where clone cost dominates; be aware mutation is 10–20× slower than mutable maps.
5. **Before acting on the concurrency numbers:** re-run the `leapfrog` contention/read-heavy groups with longer measurement windows (its 8–12% relative std in those groups is the largest uncertainty in this report), and re-measure `create` where allocator noise reached 35% relative std.
