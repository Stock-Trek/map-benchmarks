# Executive Summary — Map Implementation Benchmarks

**Date:** 2026-09-13 · **Full report:** `BENCHMARK_REPORT.md` · **Data:** Criterion `new/estimates.json` (medians)

We benchmarked 20 Rust map implementations across 112 benchmark configurations spanning creation, clone,
growth, insert, lookup, removal, iteration, clear-and-reuse, get-or-insert, key sensitivity, and concurrent
contention/synchronization/throughput workloads. This is the first run in this suite, so all figures are
absolute, within-run comparisons rather than regressions against a baseline.

**There is no single best map.** Performance separates cleanly into two largely disjoint groups. For
single-threaded work, `rustc-hash` (`FxHashMap`) is the most frequent winner (26 of 112 configurations, and
it wins 70% of the 37 configurations it can enter), driven by both a fast hash map and the Fx hasher. In the
`same-hasher` comparisons, where every map uses the same `ahash::RandomState`, the advantage shifts to
`std::collections::HashMap`, `ahash`, and `hashbrown`, which are consistently within a few percent of each
other and of the winner (these three are within 10% of the leader in 20–27 of the 64 configurations they
enter each). We
recommend `rustc-hash` when raw single-threaded speed matters, and `std`/`ahash`/`hashbrown` when a custom or
collision-resistant hasher is required. Beyond the generalists, several maps are clear specialists:
`intmap` wins integer-key lookups, misses, and removals; `indexmap` is unbeaten at iteration (all six
iterate configurations by a wide margin); and the persistent maps — `rpds-hash-trie-map`, `imbl`, and
`immutable-chunkmap` — clone entire maps in O(1) via structural sharing (rpds ~1 ns versus ~143 µs for `std`
and ~8 ms for `papaya` at 100K entries). `immutable-chunkmap` also has the cheapest empty-map constructor.

**Concurrency has a different hierarchy.** `txmap` is the most dependable generalist, winning 17
configurations across both key types and near the top in most others. `leapfrog` dominates u64 concurrent
work (18 wins, effectively sweeping u64 contention and synchronization), but it demands `Copy` keys, a fixed
value trait, and has no clone or clear, so it is not a drop-in choice. `papaya` is the clear winner for
read-only workloads with either key type; `dashmap` wins 2-thread String hot-key synchronization and `scc`
wins the 3-thread equivalents and String write-heavy throughput. Notably, `concurrent-map` — despite its
name — never places within 10% of the winner: it pays heavy per-map construction (≈8.9 ms to build 10K maps,
~3,100× the best) and its non-`Sync` EBR design forces the wrapper into an awkward per-thread-clone pattern.
`btreemap`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `imbl`, and `starshard` likewise never reach within 10% of the fastest in
any measured operation.

**Confidence and caveats.** The large majority of results (74 of 112 configurations) show a single clear
