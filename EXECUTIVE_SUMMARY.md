## What we measured

The suite compares 20 Rust map implementations — serial hash maps (std, hashbrown, ahash, rustc-hash, indexmap, hashlink, intmap, btreemap, leapfrog), concurrent maps (dashmap, scc, papaya, txmap, leapfrog, starshard, horde, crossbeam-skiplist, concurrent-map) and persistent maps (rpds, imbl, immutable-chunkmap) — across construction, insert, lookup, remove, iteration, growth, clone/copy-on-write, clear-and-reuse, key-type sensitivity, and realistic single- and multi-threaded mixed workloads. Every benchmark runs twice where supported: "out-of-the-box" (each map's default hasher) and "same-hasher" (all maps forced onto an identical ahash hasher to isolate data-structure cost from hashing cost).

## Key findings

**Single-threaded: rustc-hash is the fastest all-rounder; ahash is the fastest under a controlled hasher.** rustc-hash (Fx hasher) wins 12 of the 60 groups — insert, get-or-insert, large-map growth, clone-then-write, clear-and-reuse, and all three serial mixed-throughput workloads — usually by 10–25%. Its advantage is largely its cheap default hasher: when every map shares the same hasher, ahash, std and hashbrown become statistically tied at the top (within 1–2%), with ahash edging ahead on insert/growth. hashbrown and std are perennially within 10% of the winner and are the safe defaults. Note that rustc-hash's Fx hasher is not collision-hardened, so it should not be used on untrusted keys.

**Concurrent: it's a two-horse race between leapfrog and txmap, split by workload direction.** leapfrog wins all three contention groups (its lead grows with Zipfian skew, up to 2.4× over txmap), the read-side synchronization groups (read-only, read-mostly, read-majority, and write-majority by a hair), and 3-thread balanced throughput. txmap wins write-mostly and write-only synchronization, all three 2-thread throughput groups, and 3-thread read-heavy/write-heavy. Both beat the sharded/lock-free field (dashmap, scc, papaya, starshard, crossbeam-skiplist, concurrent-map) by 1.25× to 8.5×. For a concurrent cache or lookup-heavy service choose leapfrog; for write-heavy or transaction-like workloads choose txmap.

**Specialists dominate their niches.** indexmap is 2.4–3.7× faster at iteration than every other map (contiguous Vec storage). intmap (integer keys only) has the fastest u64 lookups and removes anywhere. The persistent maps (rpds, imbl, immutable-chunkmap) copy in ~1–4 ns regardless of size (O(1) structural sharing) but pay 4–30× on writes and copy-on-write. immutable-chunkmap has the cheapest empty-map construction. std never wins single-key operations but is within 10% of the fastest in 14 groups — a defensible default everywhere.

## Caveats

Several headline results sit on noisy measurements. rustc-hash's marquee `throughput-1-thread balanced` win shows ~15% relative std-dev; leapfrog's `get-or-insert` 3-thread win (1.19× over txmap) shows ~38% relative std-dev. Multithreaded groups dominate the high-variance list, so the ordering at the top of concurrent tables is indicative, not exact — rerun with longer measurement windows before final decisions. Persistent-map clone timings are at the timer-resolution floor and should be read via the clone-then-write numbers instead.

## Recommendation

For serial workloads, standardize on **ahash or hashbrown** (robust hashing, always near the top) and consider **rustc-hash** where keys are trusted and peak throughput matters. For concurrency, pick **leapfrog** (read/contention) or **txmap** (writes). Adopt **indexmap** for iteration-heavy code, **intmap** for integer-key lookups, and persistent maps only when cheap copying outweighs expensive writes. Add same-hasher coverage for the serial throughput family and lengthen measurement time on the concurrent groups to firm up the current top-of-table results.
