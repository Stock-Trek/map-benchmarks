# Executive Summary: Criterion Map Benchmarks

## Bottom line

There is no single best map. The right choice depends mainly on **concurrency**, **key type**, and **whether keys are trusted**.

For single-threaded work, `rustc-hash` is the performance leader: it wins **28 of the 37 serial benchmark variants** it enters, including create, clone-then-write, insert, all eight out-of-the-box growth variants, and all six serial throughput designs. The catch is security: FxHash is deterministic and has no HashDoS protection. For untrusted keys, `std`'s randomized hasher (or `ahash` where a keyed non-cryptographic hasher is acceptable) is the safer default, and the same-hasher results show `std`, `hashbrown`, and `ahash` are usually within a few percent of one another.

For concurrent work, `txmap` is the best general-purpose choice. It wins **29 of 48 concurrent groups** and is within 10% in **33 of 48**, sweeping all six 2-thread throughput variants and most String contention/synchronization cases. `leapfrog` is the specialist for **u64 `Copy` keys and values**: it wins **15 of 24** concurrent groups, including all six 3-thread u64 synchronization mixes and 3-thread u64 balanced/read-heavy throughput. `papaya` wins String read-only synchronization, including a **2.30×** lead at 3 threads, but is not a write-heavy generalist.

## What moves the needle

- **Hasher choice dominates out-of-the-box results.** `rustc-hash`'s FxHash accounts for much of its serial lead. When every map uses ahash, `hashbrown`, `std`, and `ahash` converge on most point operations. Choose FxHash when the key space is trusted; do not interpret the out-of-box gap as a pure table-structure difference.
- **Iteration is the most one-sided family.** `indexmap` is 2.0–2.6× faster than the next map at every size, because entries are stored densely in insertion order. It pays for this in point lookups, insert, and remove.
- **Integer keys are a separate regime.** `intmap` beats the best hash map by 1.34–1.45× on u64 hit/miss/remove. It cannot accept String keys, so it is a targeted optimization, not a general replacement.
- **Cheap clone is not cheap mutation.** `rpds-hash-trie-map`, `imbl`, and `immutable-chunkmap` clone in nanoseconds because of structural sharing. Once a clone is modified, path copying makes them 15.8–32.1× slower than `rustc-hash` in clone-then-write. Use them for read-mostly snapshots, not as a general copy-on-write strategy.
- **Concurrency splits by key type and read/write mix.** `txmap` sweeps 2-thread throughput and most String cases; `leapfrog` owns u64 read-heavy/skewed patterns; `papaya` owns String read-only. No single concurrent map wins all three.

## What not to conclude

- **Do not rank close results from a single run.** 1M growth is particularly noisy: the 1M String variants for `rpds-hash-trie-map`, `papaya`, and `imbl` have relative MAD of 11–25%, and the 1% gap between `rustc-hash` and `indexmap` at 1M String is inside that noise. The 3-thread String write-heavy `scc` vs `txmap` gap is also ~1%.
- **Do not compare structural clones to deep clones.** `rpds-hash-trie-map` clone is ~0.8 ns, `immutable-chunkmap` ~9 ns, `imbl` ~10 ns, and `starshard` ~90 ns; these are O(1)/shallow operations, not copies of 1K–100K entries. `clone-then-write` is the fair copy-on-write comparison.
- **Do not generalize `leapfrog` beyond `Copy` keys and values.** It cannot use String keys or non-`Copy` payloads and has no clone or clear. Its u64 wins are real but narrow.
- **Do not treat `rustc-hash` as the safe default for untrusted input.** It has no HashDoS protection. Use `std` or a keyed hasher when keys are attacker-controlled.
- **Do not infer memory footprint or tail latency.** This suite measures wall-clock medians, not bytes per entry, allocator fragmentation, or p99/p999 latency. Stability here means run-to-run spread, not production tail behavior.
