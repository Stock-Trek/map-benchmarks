# Executive Summary — Rust Map Implementation Benchmarks

**Date:** 2026-08-22 · **Source:** Criterion report (`target/criterion/`), 78 benchmark groups across 20 map implementations, median timings from `estimates.json`.

## Bottom line

The benchmark suite confirms what many of us suspected and overturns a few assumptions. For serial, single-threaded code, the hash function matters far more than the table: **`rustc-hash` (FxHashMap) wins 23 of its 34 groups outright** — every insert, lookup, remove, growth, and single-threaded mixed-throughput workload — and `ahash`/`hashbrown`/`std` become statistically interchangeable (within 1.5%) once given the same hasher. For concurrent code, **`leapfrog` is the surprise champion**: it wins every high-contention workload (hot-key contention by 1.6–4.5×, single-key synchronization by 1.1–2.6×) and 3-thread balanced/read-heavy throughput, while **`dashmap` — the most widely used concurrent map — never wins a single group** in 78, and `papaya` and `starshard` never come within 10% of a winner either.

## What the numbers say

- **Serial performance is hasher-dominated.** Under default hashers, `std`'s SipHash costs ~2.4× on small-key lookups (`std` 1.77 µs vs `ahash` 742 ns for String lookups). Under a shared hasher, `std`, `ahash`, `hashbrown`, and `hashlink` converge to within 1.5%. Anyone still shipping `std::collections::HashMap` with the default hasher for hot paths is leaving ~2× on the table.
- **`rustc-hash` is the serial speed king** — but it uses deterministic FxHash (no DoS resistance), cannot take a custom hasher, and is single-threaded. It is the right choice for hot serial code with trusted keys.
- **`leapfrog` dominates concurrency under contention.** It wins all 3 contention and 6 synchronization workloads, and gets *faster* as contention increases while every other map degrades. The catch: it requires `Copy` keys *and* `Copy` values, has no `clear()`/`Clone`, and is expensive to construct and grow (107 ms to 1M entries). Its win rate (12 of 46 groups) is the best among concurrent maps by far.
- **`txmap` is the best low-thread-count concurrent map**: it wins all 2-thread throughput groups, 2-thread concurrent get-or-insert, and 3-thread write-heavy throughput. It weakens under read-only single-key sync (8× behind `leapfrog`).
- **`indexmap` owns iteration** — 3× faster full traversal than any hash map (contiguous storage) — and also wins String-key growth under a shared hasher. It pays for that with slower insert/lookup.
- **`intmap` is the fastest integer-keyed map** (≈1.2 ns/lookup in cache), for integer-only use cases.
- **Persistent maps (`rpds`, `imbl`, `immutable-chunkmap`) deliver O(1) clones** (1–13 ns at any size vs ~140 µs for a deep-copying 100K hash map) — but clone-then-write reverses the story (10–17× slower than `ahash`), because every mutation of a shared clone pays copy-on-write. `immutable-chunkmap` also has the worst 1M-entry growth in the suite (497 ms).
- **Notable non-winners**: `dashmap` (0 wins in 78 groups), `papaya` (never within 10%; 18× degradation from read-only to write-only single-key sync), `starshard` (never within 10%; fixed 8 shards in the harness), `btreemap`/`crossbeam-skiplist` (never close; O(log n) structural costs).

## Guidance

- **Serial hot path:** `rustc-hash` (trusted keys) or `ahash`/`hashbrown` with a fast hasher. `indexmap` if you iterate, `intmap` if keys are integers.
- **Concurrent:** `leapfrog` where `Copy` keys/values are acceptable (its restriction is the single biggest limitation in the suite); `txmap` for 2-thread or write-heavy workloads; `scc` as the closest generic fallback.
- **Snapshots/versioning:** persistent maps for clone-heavy workloads — but avoid them if clones are written to.
- **Watch out:** `create`/`rustc-hash` is the noisiest measurement in the suite (std = 0.45× median), so the `create` ranking between `immutable-chunkmap` and `rustc-hash` is a statistical tie. Extreme lookup numbers come from tiny 100-key maps that fit in L1 and will not scale to large maps. No baseline/change data exists, so all results are absolute medians; treat sub-2% differences as equivalence. The `concurrent-map` numbers include a per-thread clone harness overhead and understate the raw crate.
