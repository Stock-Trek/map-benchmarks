# Executive Summary — Map Implementation Benchmarks

**Date:** 2026-09-06 · **Source:** Criterion report `target/criterion/` (median timings from `new/estimates.json`, 20 implementations × 112 benchmark configurations across 16 workloads). Full details and per-configuration tables are in [`BENCHMARK_REPORT.md`](./BENCHMARK_REPORT.md).

## Headline findings

There is no single "fastest map"; the winner depends on the arena, and the arena is determined by (a) hashing, (b) threading, and (c) whether the workload is single-shot or reuse-heavy:

- **Single-threaded, out-of-the-box: `rustc_hash` (`FxHashMap`) wins almost everything** — 26 of 37 default-hasher serial configurations, often clearly ahead (e.g. 1.1–1.4× on insert/growth, up to ~1.6× on String workloads). Most of this edge is its cheap FxHasher, not the table structure.
- **Once the hasher is equalised (same-hasher runs), `std`, `ahash` and `hashbrown` are effectively interchangeable** — within ~1–3% of each other on lookups, inserts, removes, growth (u64), get-or-insert and key-sensitivity. These three share the SwissTable design and are the safe default choice for serial use with a fixed hasher.
- **`indexmap` is the iteration and String-build specialist**: 3.4–4.2× faster than the field at iterating (contiguous entries vector) and the same-hasher leader for String-keyed insert/growth at every size (up to 1.35× over hashbrown at 1M entries). It is, however, only mid-pack for lookups and deep-copies on clone.
- **`intmap` is unbeatable for `u64`-only point operations** (lookup hit/miss/remove out-of-the-box), but does not support String keys and is slower for bulk builds.
- **Concurrency is a two-horse race: `txmap` and `leapfrog`.** `txmap` wins 35 of 48 concurrent configurations — every threaded-throughput workload except one, most contention mixes and most single-hot-key mixes — usually clearly ahead of `dashmap`/`papaya`/`scc`/`starshard` (1.1–2×). `leapfrog` (u64 keys only) takes over under heavy writer contention and highly skewed (Zipfian) u64 key distributions. `scc` edges out `txmap` on String write-heavy at 3 threads; `papaya` wins pure read-only single-key String at 3 threads; `dashmap` wins single-key String write-only at 2 threads. The ordered concurrent maps (`crossbeam-skiplist`, `concurrent-map`) never approach the leaders — `crossbeam-skiplist` trails by ~3–15× in concurrent workloads and `concurrent-map` by up to ~39×.
- **Persistent maps are a different product**: `rpds`, `imbl` and `immutable-chunkmap` clone in O(1) (~2–5 ns regardless of size — 35,000× faster than deep-copying a 100K map) and `rpds`/`imbl` construct cheaply, but their copy-on-write writes are 3–29× slower than mutable maps and they cannot `clear` (pooling). `starshard` offers O(1) COW clones *and* a shared-mutable concurrent API, but is rarely competitive on ordinary operations.

## What the numbers mean for engineering decisions

For most single-threaded products, switching from `std::collections::HashMap` to `rustc-hash` buys the largest easy win, at the cost of giving up hash-flood resistance (FxHash is not DoS-hardened) — the same-hasher results show `std`/`ahash`/`hashbrown` have no structural penalty, only a hashing one. Teams needing SipHash-grade safety should simply keep `std` or use `ahash`. Choose `indexmap` only when iteration order or iteration speed matters; choose persistent maps only when snapshot/copy semantics are the actual requirement; and choose `intmap` only for integer-keyed hot loops.

On the concurrent side, the data argue for **not defaulting to the most popular crate**: `dashmap` is the community default but finished within 10% of the fastest in only 2 of its 112 configurations. `txmap` and `leapfrog` are consistently ahead, but both need validation before adoption:

1. **Concurrent benchmarks used each map's default hasher** — `txmap` ships rapidhash and `starshard` ships FxHash, while `dashmap`/`papaya`/`scc` default to SipHash. For String keys this mixes hashing speed into the concurrency ranking, so part of `txmap`'s lead may be hashing rather than synchronisation. A shared-hasher concurrent run is needed before the ordering is treated as definitive.
2. **`concurrent-map`'s results are a harness artefact**: the crate's map is `Send`-but-not-`Sync`, so the wrapper clones a master map into per-thread storage *inside the timed region* each iteration, and threads never mutate one shared map. Its large apparent deficits (up to ~39×) should not be read as the crate's real-world performance.
3. **Noisy winners are not a concern**: only 9 of 1,403 measurements have >10% relative MAD, none of them belonging to a leading implementation; median relative MAD is 0.6%.

## Recommendations for the benchmark suite

- Add **common-hasher concurrent variants** (or force SipHash on `txmap`/`starshard`) to separate hashing from concurrency design.
- **Re-harness `concurrent-map`** (pre-clone outside the timed region, or exercise a genuinely shared instance) before drawing conclusions about it.
- Add a capacity-hinted/`with_capacity` growth run to confirm `indexmap`'s surprising String-growth advantage, and profile `horde`'s same-hasher remove win (~20–50% faster than the SwissTable maps) to confirm it is not a wrapper effect.
- Reconsider whether the ordered maps (`crossbeam-skiplist`, `btreemap`) earn their place in future runs, given they never finish near the top in any current workload.

Bottom line: for serial code the safe, well-supported recommendation is `std`/`ahash`/`hashbrown` (with `rustc-hash` when hashing can be cheap), `indexmap` for ordered/iteration-heavy tables, and the persistent trio only where snapshots matter. For concurrent code, `txmap` and `leapfrog` are the measured leaders today, but validate them with a fair-hasher run and your own contention profile before committing.
