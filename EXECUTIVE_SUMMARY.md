# Executive Summary — Rust Map Benchmark Report

## What we measured

We analysed the full Criterion report for this repository: **19 map implementations**, **15 operation families**, **112 benchmark groups** and **1,323 individual measurements**. All figures are absolute medians from `target/criterion/`; there is no baseline or historical change data. The full detail, per-operation tables and per-implementation notes are in `BENCHMARK_REPORT.md`.

The headline finding is simple: **there is no single best map.** The winner changes with the workload, the key type, and especially whether the map is used single-threaded or concurrently. The practical decision is a short list of specialists plus a small set of safe general-purpose defaults.

## Single-threaded results

The **hash-table family dominates** single-threaded work. `rustc-hash`, `ahash` and `hashbrown` form a tight leading group across insert, growth, lookup, remove and get-or-insert. `rustc-hash` has the fastest raw medians (it wins 27 of 112 groups), but it ships a fixed non-cryptographic hasher and cannot be configured or shared across threads. `ahash` is the most consistently competitive map overall — within 10% of the fastest in 29 of its 64 groups — and wins whenever the hasher is held constant. `hashbrown` is a close third (within 10% in 26 of 64 groups).

Two specialists stand out:

- **`indexmap` is the runaway winner for iteration.** Its dense backing vector is roughly 2.8× faster than the next best implementation at 100K entries, at every size and in both hasher modes.
- **Persistent maps win cloning by orders of magnitude.** `rpds-hash-trie-map` clones a 100K-entry map in ~1.3 ns through structural sharing, with `imbl` and `immutable-chunkmap` in the same order of magnitude. They then lose badly at every other operation, so they are only appropriate when cheap snapshots are the priority.

For **integer keys**, `intmap` is the clearest single-threaded lookup/remove winner, but it cannot hold string keys. The **ordered maps** (`btreemap`, `crossbeam-skiplist`) never reach within 10% of the fastest on any measured operation — that is expected. They are ordering tools, not speed tools, and the benchmark set does not measure the range-scan behaviour that justifies them.

## Concurrency rewrites the leaderboard

Nothing from the single-threaded leaderboard participates in the concurrent benchmarks, because those maps lack interior mutability. The concurrent winners are workload-dependent:

- **`txmap`** wins 2-thread throughput and most mixed/String contention (20 group wins overall) and is the best general-purpose concurrent choice, at the cost of a very expensive construction and slow single-threaded performance.
- **`leapfrog`** sweeps every u64 synchronization and 3-thread contention case (18 wins), but requires `Copy` keys and cannot be cloned.
- **`papaya`** is the only winner of the String read-only synchronization group.
- **`scc`** takes the 3-thread write-heavy throughput cases; **`dashmap`** several String write-heavy synchronization cases.

`crossbeam-skiplist` and `starshard` were never within 10% of the winner in any concurrent group.

## An important confound: the default hasher

The "out-of-the-box" numbers measure the whole package (map *plus* its shipped hasher), and the default hasher can swamp the data structure. The clearest example: in the u64 lookup-hit benchmark, `std` (SipHash) is 7.3× slower than `intmap` out of the box, but within 1% of the fastest once all maps share the same hasher. This also explains `ahash`'s extremely slow empty-map construction (`new()` builds a fresh random state per map) and why the concurrent comparison is currently confounded: those benchmarks have no shared-hasher variant. Conclusions about a *data structure* should come from the `same-hasher` groups; conclusions about a *ready-to-use map* should come from `out-of-the-box`.

## Measurement quality

The data is generally tight. **91% of measurements have a median-absolute-deviation at or below 3% of the median, 97% at or below 5%, and only two of 1,323 measurements exceed 10%.** Those two are 3-thread String synchronization runs for `scc` and `dashmap`, which are the only areas where the ordering of second and third place should be treated as provisional. A few fast maps show high `std_dev` but low MAD (`rustc-hash` on `Create`, `hashbrown`/`ahash` on 100K `Clone`), i.e. occasional slow samples rather than systematic noise; the median/MAD conclusions in the report are unaffected.

## Recommendations

For most teams the default guidance is: **`ahash` when keys may be untrusted or you need a configurable hasher; `rustc-hash` when raw single-threaded speed matters and the key space is safe; `indexmap` when you iterate; `rpds`/`imbl` when you need cheap persistent snapshots; `intmap` for integer keys; and `txmap` (general) or `leapfrog` (integer keys, hot-key synchronisation) for concurrent sharing.** Choose `btreemap` only when ordering is a hard requirement.

Two follow-ups would materially improve confidence. First, **add `&self` mutation traits to the `horde` wrapper** so this concurrent map appears in concurrent groups — it is currently the largest coverage gap. Second, **add a shared-hasher variant to the concurrent benchmarks**, because today they compare default-hasher packages rather than map designs. Longer, CPU-pinned runs of the 3-thread String synchronization groups would also firm up the noisiest results. Everything here is a single run on a single machine and should be re-measured on target hardware before a production decision.
