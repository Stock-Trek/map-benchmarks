# Executive Summary — Rust Map Implementation Benchmarks

**Prepared for:** Engineering leadership
**Supporting detail:** [`BENCHMARK_REPORT.md`](./BENCHMARK_REPORT.md)
**Data basis:** 1,323 Criterion.rs measurements covering **112 benchmark variants** and **19 map implementations**. We report the median time for a fixed unit of work (lower is better). A contender within **10%** of the fastest is treated as "practically equivalent." No baselines or historical data exist — these are absolute, current measurements.

---

## Why this matters

Map choice affects latency, throughput and memory across many services. This study replaces folklore ("`dashmap` is fast", "`ahash` is fast") with measured rankings for both single-threaded and concurrent workloads, and — importantly — separates the effect of the **hasher** from the effect of the **map data structure**.

## Bottom line

1. **Single-threaded default: `rustc-hash` (`FxHashMap`).** It is fastest in **26 of 64** serial variants, dominating inserts, growth and get-or-insert. **Caveat:** its FxHash hasher is *not* collision/DoS-resistant, so it is for trusted keys. Where inputs are untrusted, use **`std` HashMap** — it wins 8 variants and is within 10% of the best in 18.
2. **Much of `rustc-hash`'s apparent lead is the hasher, not the map.** When every map is forced to use the same hasher, **`std`, `hashbrown` and `ahash` converge** (within 10% of the best in 18–23 variants each). We should not claim a data-structure win where a hasher win was measured.
3. **Iteration-heavy workloads: `indexmap`.** It wins all six full-iteration benchmarks, and by **2.8–3.4×** — contiguous storage is decisive. It is only worth the dependency where iteration frequency justifies it.
4. **Concurrent default: `txmap`.** Fastest in **33 of 48** concurrent variants and within 10% in 39. **`leapfrog`** is the specialist for `u64` keys (all 12 of its wins) and single hot keys. **`scc`** takes a few String write-heavy cases. There is no single concurrent winner independent of key type.
5. **Snapshot/persistent use cases: `rpds-hash-trie-map`** clones in ~2.2 ns (O(1) structural sharing); `immutable-chunkmap` and `imbl` are also cheap to clone. These are *not* competitive on mutation or iteration and should be treated as a separate category.

## Reliability of the numbers

- **`dashmap` is the noisiest concurrent map** (up to 34% std_dev/mean); treat its rank as unreliable, especially as a close second.
- **`leapfrog`'s narrow hot-key wins** over `txmap` are often within ~7% and should be re-measured before adoption.
- **1M-entry growth results are noisy across all maps** (allocation/OS effects). Do not over-index on those rankings.
- **`rpds` iteration** is very noisy (0.27 MAD/median) but slow there, so it does not change any conclusion.
- **Sub-10 ns clone and sub-300 ns lookups** sit near the timer's noise floor; they are directionally correct but should be confirmed with unit-time benchmarks before being quoted.

## Scope gaps and risks

- **Concurrency was measured at only 2 and 3 threads**; rankings may shift at high core counts, particularly for sharded vs. fine-grained maps.
- **Ordered maps** (`btreemap`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `immutable-chunkmap`) and maps without configurable hashers (`intmap`, `rustc-hash`) cannot participate in the fair "same-hasher" comparison, so their head-to-head numbers are less controlled.
- **Three implementations were excluded** (`concread`, `flurry`, `concurrent-map`) — the first two for being far slower, the last because it is not `Sync`. This is a deliberate scope decision, not a claim that these maps are universally unsuitable.
- **Key/value shapes are limited** to `u64` and `String` keys with `u64` values. Integer-only (`intmap`) and `Copy`-key-only (`leapfrog`) maps are consequently under-represented.

## Proposed next steps

1. **Adopt `txmap` as the concurrent default and `std` as the safe serial default**, with `rustc-hash` allowed only for trusted, integer-like keys and `indexmap` only for iteration-dominated code paths.
2. **Re-run the noisy variants** (1M growth, `dashmap` synchronization, `leapfrog` contention/hot-key, `rpds` iteration) to firm up marginal decisions.
3. **Extend concurrency testing** to higher thread counts (e.g. 8–16) to validate scaling, not just absolute ordering.
4. **Add a controlled "same fast hasher on every table" run** plus a dedicated ordered-map comparison to remove the remaining confounds.
5. **Add an amortized/unit-time benchmark** for near-zero operations (create, clone, small lookups) to improve measurement confidence.
