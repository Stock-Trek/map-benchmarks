## The headline

**No single map wins everything — the field splits cleanly into a serial tier and a concurrent tier, with one clear leader in each.** For multi-threaded workloads, **`txmap` is the fastest in 41 of the 48 concurrent benchmark groups** (contention, synchronization, concurrent throughput across 2–3 threads, u64 and String keys), usually by a margin of more than 10% — with `leapfrog` competitive on heavily skewed 3-thread u64 workloads, and `scc`/`papaya` taking single write-heavy/read-only corner cases. ## What matters for consumers

For real applications, the practical choice comes down to: **`txmap` if you need shared concurrent access; `rustc-hash` (or `ahash`) if you are single-threaded and can accept its fixed Fx hasher; `std`/`hashbrown`/`ahash` are interchangeable when you bring your own hasher** — and that hasher choice matters enormously: `std`'s default SipHash costs ~6× on u64 lookups (1.79 µs vs 295 ns per 100 lookups with a cheap hasher). `indexmap` is the right call when iteration order or full-map walks matter (2–3× faster iteration than any competitor). For copy-heavy or persistent-semantics workloads, the structural-sharing maps (`rpds-hash-trie-map`, `imbl`) make cloning essentially free — but any workload that writes to the copies should use `rustc-hash`/`ahash` instead.

## Caveats worth knowing

- **Serial and concurrent leadership are mutually exclusive in practice:** `txmap` is mid-pack or slow in serial benchmarks, and the fastest serial maps are not concurrent at all. There is no "one map for everything" in this data.
- **Several headline numbers rest on noisy measurements.** Short benchmarks (sub-200 µs) show 10–34% relative median-absolute-deviation even for the leaders (`rustc-hash`, `ahash`, `hashbrown`, `std`), and the concurrent benchmarks show 13–30% relative std-dev for `dashmap` and `concurrent-map`. Differences under ~5% between close contenders (e.g. the `ahash`/`std`/`hashbrown` same-hasher cluster) should be treated as noise-level unless reproduced.
- **The `concurrent-map` results should be interpreted with care:** its wrapper clones the map into a thread-local per thread, so workers operate on private copies — the numbers measure per-thread private-map throughput, not shared-map contention.
- **Two gaps in coverage:** `concread` and `flurry` are wrapped but were excluded as too slow (no data), and the README-documented concurrent get-or-insert benchmark was not found in the Criterion data. Re-running those would complete the picture.
- All conclusions use median point estimates, as no baseline/change data exists; only absolute timings were available.
