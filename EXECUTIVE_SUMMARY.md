# Executive Summary — Map Implementation Benchmarks

**Date:** 2026-08-20 · Source: Criterion.rs medians in `target/criterion/` (94 benchmark cells, 19 implementations, current absolute measurements only — no baseline data).

## Bottom line

- **For single-threaded use, `rustc-hash` is the fastest map in this suite** — it wins every serial workload benchmark (insert, lookup-hit, remove, get-or-insert, clear-and-reuse, and all mixed serial workloads), typically 10–30% faster than the runner-up and up to ~45% faster on get-or-insert. Its advantage is almost entirely its FxHasher: when every map is forced onto the same `ahash::RandomState` hasher, `std::collections::HashMap`, `hashbrown::HashMap`, and `ahash::AHashMap` are statistically indistinguishable (within 1–4%), so users who need a portable, well-known implementation should pick any of those three with no practical penalty. `rustc-hash` does not support custom hashers, which is why it disappears from the same-hasher comparison.
- **For concurrent use, `txmap` and `leapfrog` are the class leaders** — between them they win 26 of 27 concurrent workload cells and 8 of 9 concurrent get-or-insert cells. `txmap` dominates at 2–3 threads and read-heavy mixes; `leapfrog` tends to win at 4 threads and on large write-heavy maps. `dashmap`, `scc`, `starshard`, and `crossbeam-skiplist` never win a concurrent workload benchmark; `dashmap` and `scc` typically trail the leaders by ~1.5–2× and `starshard`/`crossbeam-skiplist` by ~4–11×. On single-threaded work the concurrent maps pay a 2–16× penalty vs the serial leader (`txmap`/`dashmap`/`horde` are the cheapest at ~2×, `papaya` and `crossbeam-skiplist` the most expensive at ~6× and ~16×) — the serial/concurrent split is the sharpest divide in the data.
- **Structural specialization beats tuning:** `indexmap` is the decisive iteration winner (its contiguous storage iterates at ~0.4 ns/element, roughly 2–3× faster than any hash table); persistent maps (`rpds`, `imbl`, `immutable-chunkmap`) make clone O(1) and map creation essentially free, at the cost of being far slower at mutation; and `horde` — a lock-free concurrent map — unexpectedly wins all same-hasher remove benchmarks and is within 10% on same-hasher inserts, making it the most interesting "why is this fast?" follow-up.

## Caveats that matter

Concurrent measurements are the weak spot of this dataset. Nearly every noisy cell (relative median-absolute-deviation above 10%, relative std-dev up to 190%) is a 4-thread benchmark, and several top-of-table results — `leapfrog` at 4 threads, `txmap` at 4 threads, `scc` and `papaya` in their single 100K/threads-4 wins — rest on those noisy measurements. The 2–3-thread results are clean and firm. With only 1 s warm-up / 2 s measurement per benchmark and no baseline data, we recommend re-running the threads-4 cells with longer measurement time before making procurement-grade decisions, and treating any sub-10% difference (e.g. the std/ahash/hashbrown tie) as equivalent. Two apparent outliers are structural artifacts rather than real wins: rpds's 2 ns clone is an O(1) refcount bump from structural sharing (not a deep copy), and immutable-chunkmap's ~0.3 ns "create" measures allocation laziness of empty persistent maps.
