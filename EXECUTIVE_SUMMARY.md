# Executive Summary: Rust Map Benchmark Results

**What was measured.** The suite covers **19 map implementations** across **112 benchmark groups** (1,323 individual measurements): construction, insert, upsert, hit/miss lookups, removal, iteration, growth to 1M entries, clearing and reuse, clone and clone-then-write, and mixed workloads on 1–3 threads including contention on a single hot key. Everything here comes from one Criterion run on one machine: there is no saved baseline, so this is a comparison of implementations against each other, not a regression report. The full analysis, including per-group winners and caveats, is in `BENCHMARK_REPORT.md`.

**There is no overall winner; the decision breaks at the concurrency boundary.** Single-threaded, the four conventional hash tables (`rustc-hash`, `ahash`, `hashbrown`, `std`) are within a few percent of each other once they use the same hasher, and `rustc-hash` wins 25 of 64 serial groups out of the box. That lead, however, is mostly the hasher: swapping `std`'s SipHash-1-3 for the shared hasher turns a 13.8 ns lookup into 2.76 ns, a 5× swing, and collapses the top three tables into a three-way tie. `indexmap` wins all six iteration groups by 2.6–3.4× and every String-keyed growth variant with a shared hasher; `intmap` wins every integer-key point operation by 1.2–1.5× but cannot accept non-integer keys; `immutable-chunkmap` and the persistent maps win construction and cloning by orders of magnitude, then lose clone-then-write by 10–26× because each post-clone write path-copies. The practical serial default is `rustc-hash` (or `ahash` where DoS resistance or a swappable hasher matters), with `indexmap` for scans and `intmap` for integer keys.

**Concurrently, `txmap` is the strongest map in the suite but the evidence needs independent confirmation.** `txmap` wins 37 of the 48 concurrent groups, with `leapfrog` second (8 wins, all on u64) and `scc`/`papaya` taking single groups each. The margins matter: `txmap` leads the runner-up by up to 1.59× in mixed workloads, but the lead shrinks with thread count, and at 3 threads `leapfrog` overtakes it on u64 read-heavy throughput (371 µs vs 411 µs) and on heavily skewed u64 contention (366 µs vs 758 µs). `dashmap` is a consistent 1.2–1.8× behind in mixed workloads and `starshard`/`crossbeam-skiplist` trail by 1.4–14×. Two caveats temper this: concurrent groups contain only 6–7 of the 19 implementations (most maps cannot be mutated through `&self`), and `txmap` is authored by the organisation that maintains this suite, was measured only in its default mutex configuration, and was not exercised through its transactional API. We should treat the concurrent ranking as a strong hypothesis, not a settled result.

**The measurements themselves are clean, with a short list of exceptions.** Median relative MAD is 0.58% across the suite and only 2.7% of measurements exceed 5%. The exceptions are specific and identified: `rpds-hash-trie-map`'s 100K iteration (34.6% MAD, and a 1.88× gap between two variants of an operation that does no hashing), `imbl`'s 100K/1M growth (24.2%/9.7%), `dashmap`'s 2-thread single-key groups (12.5–12.8%) and `crossbeam-skiplist`'s 3-thread read-only group (15.4%). Sub-10 ns clone results and sub-2 ns per-map construction results are at the edge of the timer's resolution and should be read as ties. Separately, occasional slow samples inflate the *means* of several fast clone and remove measurements by 20–40% — which is why the report ranks on medians throughout.

**The capability gaps are as decision-relevant as the timings.** The wrapper code shows why the comparison matrix is incomplete: `leapfrog` requires `Copy` keys and values (so it has no String-key data at all), `intmap` is integer-only, `horde`'s write API takes `&mut self` (so the most consistent serial map cannot enter any concurrent benchmark), and `immutable-chunkmap`, `rpds-hash-trie-map` and `leapfrog` have no `clear`, while `horde` and `rpds-hash-trie-map` have no upsert API. Three further crates in `Cargo.toml` (`concread`, `concurrent-map`, `flurry`) contribute no data.
