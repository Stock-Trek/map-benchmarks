# Executive Summary — Map Implementation Benchmark Results

**Date:** 2026-08-21 · **Source:** Criterion reports in `target/criterion/` (1,448 measurements across 124 benchmark groups, 19 map implementations, single run — no change/baseline data)

## The headline

Across every single-threaded benchmark, **`rustc-hash` (FxHashMap) is the fastest implementation** — it wins 26 of the 37 out-of-the-box single-threaded groups (bulk insert, lookup hit/miss, remove, get-or-insert, create, clone-then-write, clear-and-reuse, and all serial mixed workloads), usually by a clear margin. The only out-of-the-box serial groups it does not win are the specialized ones: iteration (indexmap), clone (rpds), and a handful of single insert/lookup-miss/remove/clear-and-reuse groups where hashbrown/ahash/hashlink edge it out by small margins. Its FxHash hasher is simply very cheap for small keys. The caveat is that FxHash is not collision/DoS-resistant; when a robust hasher is required, `ahash` and `hashbrown` are effectively tied with `std::HashMap` (and with each other) — the gap between them is ~1–2%, i.e. practically equivalent.

In every multi-threaded benchmark, the winner comes from one of two implementations: **`txmap`** (dominant at 2 threads and in read-heavy/cache-entry workloads) and **`leapfrog`** (dominant at 3–4 threads and larger maps). Together they won 62 of 63 concurrent groups and are within 10% of the fastest in ~31% of all groups each. The only exception is a single `scc` win (get-or-insert 100K, 4 threads) that sits inside the noisiest measurement of the entire suite and should be treated as unconfirmed. The other concurrent maps (dashmap, papaya, starshard, crossbeam-skiplist) never won a group and typically trail txmap/leapfrog by 1.5–3× (crossbeam-skiplist by ~10×).

This serial-vs-concurrent split is the single most important pattern: there is no one-size-fits-all winner, and picking a map family without knowing the threading model is the biggest decision risk.

## Notable specialty winners

- **`indexmap`** wins all iteration benchmarks by a wide margin (e.g. 35.6 µs vs ~117 µs for std at 100K entries) thanks to contiguous entry storage.
- **`rpds`** clones a 100K-entry map in ~1.4 ns — because clone is O(1) structural sharing, not a deep copy. If you want snapshot semantics it is exceptional; if you want independent copies, it is not what it appears to be. Its mutation and iteration are 3–70× slower than the leaders.
- **`horde`** is the fastest remover (all same-hasher remove groups) and competitive on large same-hasher inserts, but requires `&mut` writes, which excluded it from the shared-reference concurrent benchmarks.

## Stability caveats (read before acting on close rankings)

The concurrent benchmarks, especially the 100,000-entry / 4-thread groups, are noisy: 92 of 1,448 measurements (6%) show high relative dispersion, and several winning measurements have std/median between 0.3 and 2.4. The txmap↔leapfrog ordering at 4 threads, and scc's single win (get-or-insert 100K, 4 threads), sit inside these noisy groups and should be re-measured with longer runs before being treated as definitive. Single-threaded results are stable and trustworthy.
