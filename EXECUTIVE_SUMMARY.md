# Executive Summary — Map Implementation Benchmarks

**Date:** 2026-08-20 · **Source:** Criterion.rs report in `target/criterion/` (single run, no baseline)

## Headline findings

Nineteen map implementations were benchmarked across 124 benchmark groups covering 14 workloads (create, insert, lookup hit/miss, remove, clear-and-reuse, clone, clone-then-write, iterate, get-or-insert, mixed read/write workloads with uniform and Zipfian key distributions) at map sizes of 1 000–100 000 entries, single- and multi-threaded (2–4 threads), both with each map's default hasher and with a common `ahash` hasher.

**There is no universal winner — the field splits cleanly by use case:**

- **Single-threaded work: `rustc-hash` (FxHashMap) wins essentially everything.** It is fastest in all 9 serial mixed workloads, all insert/lookup-hit/clear-and-reuse/create groups, and most remove/lookup-miss/get-or-insert groups (29 fastest groups overall). hashbrown is the closest consistent alternative (within ~10–20 %), and ahash/std/hashbrown are within a few percent of each other whenever the same hasher is used. Caveat: part of rustc-hash's edge is the deliberately weak-but-fast Fx hasher; it should not be chosen over ahash/hashbrown for untrusted or adversarial keys.
- **Concurrent work: `leapfrog` (LeapMap) dominates.** It is fastest in 47 of 124 groups overall and wins the majority of 2–4-thread get-or-insert, balanced, read-heavy, and Zipfian workloads, usually by 1.4–2×. Two notable exceptions: `txmap` wins the small (1 000-entry) concurrent groups, and `dashmap` wins large write-heavy Zipfian workloads (100 000 entries). `scc` wins at 100 000 entries / 4 threads, with dashmap and papaya within a few percent.
- **Specialists:** `indexmap` is ~2.5× faster than everything else at iteration (contiguous storage); `rpds-hash-trie-map` (persistent) clones in ~1 ns regardless of size — an O(1) copy-on-write clone — at the price of being 8–270× slower at mutation.
- **Hasher choice dominates table choice.** When every map is forced to use the same `ahash::RandomState`, ahash, std, hashbrown and hashlink are statistically indistinguishable (within ~1–5 %) for lookup, insert and get-or-insert. Buying a "better map" is secondary to choosing a good hasher for your key domain.

## What to act on

1. **Validate leapfrog's 100 000-entry / 4-thread get-or-insert collapse** (6.55 ms vs scc's 1.34 ms) — the only case where the concurrent champion degrades dramatically; worth understanding before relying on it at scale.
2. **Re-run the 4-thread concurrent suite with longer measurement windows.** The 2-second window produced relative std. deviations up to 104 % (leapfrog, several groups) and median-abs-deviation up to 64 %; rankings at 4 threads are directionally reliable only where margins are large. Serial measurements are very stable (mostly <2 % MAD).
3. **Mind the API constraints hidden by the benchmark harness:** leapfrog requires `Copy` keys (ineligible for `String` keys entirely), horde and others mutate only through `&mut`, rpds is not `Send`/`Sync`, and concurrent-map is `Send` but not `Sync`. The concurrent results therefore reflect a specific, `Copy`-key-friendly slice of the design space.
4. **No baseline exists** — all conclusions are relative to a single run of absolute medians; treat fine-grained rankings (sub-5 % differences) as indicative, not definitive.

**Bottom line:** for a single default recommendation, use **rustc-hash** single-threaded (with ahash/hashbrown if key security matters) and **leapfrog** concurrent where `Copy` keys are acceptable — otherwise **dashmap** (balanced/write-heavy) or **scc** (large, 4 threads). For iteration, **indexmap**; for snapshot/copy-on-write semantics, **rpds-hash-trie-map**.
