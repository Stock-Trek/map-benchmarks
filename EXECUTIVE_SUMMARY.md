# Executive Summary

## Map Benchmark Results — Key Findings

We benchmarked 19 Rust map implementations across 94 Criterion benchmark groups covering construction, cloning, insert, lookup (hit/miss), get-or-insert, remove, iteration, clear-and-reuse (pooling), key-type sensitivity (u64 and String keys), and realistic mixed workloads (balanced / read-heavy / write-heavy), both single- and multi-threaded (2–4 threads). Benchmarks were run in two modes: "out-of-the-box" (each map's own default hasher) and "same-hasher" (a shared `ahash::RandomState`), so results separate map-table performance from hasher choice.

**Two implementations dominate, in different domains.** `txmap` is the strongest concurrent map — fastest in 26 of 94 groups, including 21 of 27 concurrent workload groups — with `leapfrog` its only consistent challenger (winning most 4-thread cases, where the margins are within the measured noise). `rustc-hash` is the dominant single-threaded map, fastest in 24 of the 34 out-of-the-box groups it can participate in, across insert, lookup, remove, clear-and-reuse, get-or-insert and all three serial workloads. A significant part of rustc-hash's edge is its FxHasher: when a common hasher is forced, `std`, `ahash` and `hashbrown` (which share the same underlying hashbrown table design) are effectively tied at the top of every single-threaded operation, within ~2–5% of each other. For most single-threaded workloads there is therefore no meaningful difference between these three once hashing is held constant — and `std` additionally provides SipHash's DoS resistance at no measurable cost under a common hasher.

**Specialists matter.** `indexmap` is the iteration outlier, 2.8–4.2× faster than the field due to its contiguous storage (though its `swap_remove` reorders entries). `horde` is clearly the fastest at remove under a common hasher (17–34% ahead). `immutable-chunkmap` is fastest at empty-map construction, and the persistent maps (`rpds`, `immutable-chunkmap`, `imbl`) clone in nanoseconds because they share structure — a fundamentally different, cheaper clone semantics than deep copies, where `hashlink` (5.8 ms) and `papaya` (8.6 ms) at 100K entries are the expensive outliers.

**Key sensitivity:** hashing cost dominates lookups — String(16)/String(128) lookups cost ~3.3×/~8.7× a u64 lookup regardless of map; `ahash` edges ahead on String keys, with `std`/`hashbrown` close behind.

**Measurement quality:** serial results are very stable (winner MAD < 3%) and trustworthy. Concurrent results — especially 4-thread/100K groups — are noisy (MAD up to 87%, std/median up to 111%), so the txmap/leapfrog/scc 4-thread ordering should be treated as approximate ties rather than definitive rankings.

**Recommendations:** for a general single-threaded map, rustc-hash (if fast, non-DoS-resistant hashing is acceptable) or std/ahash/hashbrown (if hashing is controlled or must be DoS-resistant); for concurrent use, txmap (leapfrog at 4 threads); indexmap for iteration-heavy code; horde for removal-heavy code; rpds/immutable-chunkmap/imbl where cheap snapshot-style cloning is required. Two follow-ups would materially sharpen conclusions: a same-hasher comparison that feeds FxHasher into hashbrown/ahash to isolate rustc-hash's table from its hasher, and longer/repeated measurement of 4-thread txmap-vs-leapfrog to resolve margins currently inside the noise floor.
