# Executive Summary: Rust Map Benchmark Analysis

This analysis covers all current Criterion results in `target/criterion/` for the
map-benchmarks suite: **19 map implementations, 15 operations, 112 benchmark groups and
1,323 individual measurements**. All figures come from the benchmarks' own
`new/estimates.json` median estimates; no baselines or re-runs were used. A detailed
breakdown is in [`BENCHMARK_REPORT.md`](./BENCHMARK_REPORT.md).

**There is no single best map — the concurrency model is the dominant decision.** The
implementations that dominate single-threaded work cannot be mutated through a shared
reference and are entirely absent from the concurrent benchmarks, and the fastest
concurrent map is essentially never the fastest on a single thread. `txmap` is the
standout concurrent performer, fastest in **37 of 48** concurrent groups and within 10%
of the fastest in **43 of 48**. On the serial side `rustc-hash` (`FxHashMap`) is fastest
in **25 of 64** serial groups and the fastest or near-fastest across most write and mixed
workloads, but it uses a fixed, non-cryptographic hasher and cannot be shared across
threads.

**The serial results contain several operation-specific specialists.** `indexmap` wins
**all six** `iterate` variants and is strong on large `String` growth. `intmap` is
decisively fastest for integer-keyed lookups and removals (180 ns hit, 143 ns miss,
554 ns remove) but only accepts integer keys. `horde` is fastest in 3 of 4 `remove`
variants. Most dramatically, `rpds-hash-trie-map` clones a 100K-entry map in **1.76 ns**
thanks to structural sharing, versus roughly 62 µs for a deep copy — but it never wins a
write operation. The persistent maps (`rpds`, `immutable-chunkmap`, `imbl`) should be
selected for cheap snapshots, not for raw throughput.

**For teams that want a safe general-purpose default rather than a specialist, the
hash-table implementations are remarkably consistent.** `hashbrown`, `ahash` and `std`
are rarely the fastest but land within 10% of it in **26, 24 and 20 of 64** serial groups
respectively. `std` in particular rises to the top of many lookup and get-or-insert
groups once all maps are compared with the same hasher. This exposes an important
confound: much of `rustc-hash`'s out-of-the-box lead comes from its FxHasher rather than
its table. Judgements about the *data structure* should be taken from the `same-hasher`
comparisons; the `out-of-the-box` comparisons describe realistic default configurations.

**Concurrent behaviour is dominated by `txmap`.** Beyond its 37 wins, the only other
concurrent maps that take first place are `leapfrog` (8 wins, restricted to `Copy` keys
such as `u64`), `scc` (2) and `papaya` (1, the 3-thread `String` read-only case).
`dashmap` is within 10% only once; `starshard` and `crossbeam-skiplist` are never within
10%. This is a wide margin, not a statistical tie, and it holds across contention,
synchronization and multi-threaded throughput workloads.

**Measurement quality is good overall, with a few known soft spots.** The median
relative MAD across all 1,323 measurements is **0.72%** (90th percentile 3.44%). The
highest-variance areas are `clear-and-reuse` at 1K entries (heavy-tailed `std_dev/median`
of 60–72% for `ahash`, `hashbrown` and `rustc-hash`), 1M-entry `growth` (8–15% MAD for
`papaya`, `imbl`, `crossbeam-skiplist` and `indexmap`), and a handful of groups where
`rustc-hash` is fastest but variation is 8–11%. `rpds` clone has 8–11% relative MAD, but
at a 1.76 ns median the absolute jitter is negligible. These caveats do not overturn any
ranking in the report, but the `clear-and-reuse` and 1M-`growth` medians should be
re-measured before being treated as precise.

**Practical recommendations.** Pick by concurrency model first: `txmap` for shared
mutable state, with `leapfrog` for `u64` read-heavy work and `scc`/`papaya` for narrow
read/write mixes. For single-threaded code, default to `hashbrown`/`ahash`/`std` unless a
specialist clearly wins the dominant operation (`rustc-hash` for writes, `indexmap` for
iteration/order, `intmap` for integer lookups, `horde` for removal, `rpds` for snapshot
cloning). Finally, note the coverage caveats: only `u64` and `String` keys (lengths 16,
32, 128) with `u64` values were measured, concurrent runs use only 2–3 pinned threads,
no tail latency (p95/p99) is available, and `concread`, `flurry` and `concurrent_map`
were excluded from the suite for the reasons documented in the README.
