# Executive Summary

## Bottom line

This benchmark suite compares **19 Rust map implementations across 112 workload variants and 1,323 Criterion measurements**. It is an absolute comparison of the current run only; there is no baseline/change data, so the results say “which map is faster in this harness,” not “which map improved.”

There is **no overall winner**. The fastest choice depends on whether the workload is serial or concurrent, the key type, the read/write mix, and whether the map must support a custom hasher, cheap snapshots, or ordered iteration. The strongest general-purpose signals are:

- **Serial, default hasher:** `rustc-hash` is the most frequent winner (24 of the 37 serial variants it enters) and is the best default when its fixed FxHasher is acceptable.
- **Serial, shared hasher:** `ahash` and `hashbrown` are the most consistently close alternatives. `ahash` is within 10% of the winner in 28 of 64 applicable serial groups; `hashbrown` is within 10% in 23 of 64. `std` is within 10% in 16 of 64 and is a genuine winner in several same-hasher clear-and-reuse and lookup cases.
- **Concurrent mixed workloads:** `txmap` is the broad default. It wins 22 of 48 concurrent variants, including 11 of 12 concurrent throughput variants, and its median slowdown across all concurrent groups is only 1.05×.
- **Concurrent `u64` contention / single-hot-key:** `leapfrog` is the specialist. It wins all 12 `u64` synchronization variants and 4 of 6 `u64` contention variants, but it only supports `Copy` keys and has no clone or clear wrapper.
- **Concurrent `String` synchronization at 2 threads:** `dashmap` wins five of six variants. At 3 threads, `txmap` takes most mixed/write-heavy cases and `papaya` takes read-only.

## Serial results

Serial workloads are dominated by `rustc-hash` when each crate uses its default hasher. The exception is iteration: `indexmap` wins all six iterate variants, by about 3.2× at 1K, 3.0× at 10K, and 1.8× at 100K. That is the clearest specialization in the dataset: if full-map traversal is the dominant operation, `indexmap` is in a different class from the hash-table scans.

Other serial specialists are equally narrow but valuable:

- **`intmap`** is the best integer-key lookup/removal map: 158.6 ns for a 100-hit lookup iteration and 121.0 ns for a 100-miss iteration on `u64`, and it wins the out-of-the-box `u64` removal variant at 494.8 ns per 100 removals. It only supports integer keys.
- **`horde`** wins three of four removal variants (6.69 µs per 100 String removals out of the box, 4.85 µs with a common hasher) and the same-hasher `u64` growth cases. However, its benchmark wrapper is `&mut`-only and has no get-or-insert, so it is not a concurrent recommendation.
- **`rpds-hash-trie-map`** is the clone/snapshot specialist: a clone takes **4.3 ns** at 1K, 10K, and 100K, consistent with structural sharing. But persistent maps pay for it on mutation: clone-then-write at 100K takes 4.54 ms for `rpds`, 4.09 ms for `immutable-chunkmap`, and 2.70 ms for `imbl`, versus 272.01 µs for `rustc-hash`. Cheap snapshots are valuable only when the post-clone write set is small or the clone is read-mostly.

The same-hasher results are important because they remove the “good default hasher” advantage. In those groups, the top four maps are frequently within 1–5% on lookups and misses, and the differences are often smaller than the practical significance of API fit or dependency policy. A team already standardized on `std`, `ahash`, or `hashbrown` usually should not rewrite for a few percent on a microbenchmark.

## Concurrent results

`txmap` is the only concurrent map that is both broadly competitive and a frequent winner. It wins 7 of 12 contention variants, 11 of 12 concurrent throughput variants, and 4 of 24 synchronization variants, with a 1.05× median slowdown across all 48 concurrent groups. That makes it the safe first choice for a general concurrent mixed workload.

The other concurrent winners are specialists:

- **`leapfrog`** wins 17 of the 24 `u64` variants it enters, including every `u64` synchronization case. Its win rate is high because it only enters the key type and workload shapes where its design is strongest.
- **`dashmap`** is the `String` 2-thread synchronization specialist, winning five of six 2-thread `String<32>` synchronization variants.
- **`papaya`** wins the 3-thread `String<32>` read-only synchronization case; **`crossbeam-skiplist`** wins the 2-thread `String<32>` read-only case.
- **`scc`** never wins but is within 10% in 4 of 48 concurrent groups; **`starshard`** has no wins or close finishes. Neither is a competitive default in this dataset.

The practical concurrent decision is therefore: start with `txmap`; replace it with `leapfrog` only when keys are `Copy`/`u64` and the workload is high-contention; use `dashmap` for 2-thread `String` synchronization; and use `papaya` or `crossbeam-skiplist` only for narrow read-only cases.

## Stability and confidence

The measurements are mostly tight. Only **65 of 1,323 measurements (4.9%)** have high variance by the report's flags (MAD/median >5% or CV >10%). No measurement exceeds 20% MAD/median, and no measurement exceeds 50% CV. The noisy tail is concentrated in 100K `String<32>` growth, 3-thread `String<32>` synchronization, and the small 1K clone/clone-then-write cases. Several winning rows in the first two families have 6–7% MAD, so their exact margins should be treated as approximate even though the direction of the win is clear.

The headline conclusions are robust because they rest on >10% gaps or repeated wins across variants. The fragile conclusions are the 1–5% same-hasher lookup/miss differences and any close call in a row with high variance. Those should be treated as ties unless corroborated by another operation or a repeat run.

## Recommended actions

1. **Choose by dominant operation, not by an overall ranking.** Use `rustc-hash` for serial default-hasher workloads, `ahash`/`hashbrown`/`std` when the hasher is shared, `indexmap` for iteration/order, `intmap` for integer-key lookups, `horde` for removal-heavy serial work, and `rpds` only when cheap read-mostly snapshots dominate.
2. **Start concurrent work with `txmap`.** Move to `leapfrog` for `u64` high-contention cases, `dashmap` for 2-thread `String` synchronization, and `papaya`/`crossbeam-skiplist` for read-only niches.
3. **Do not treat excluded maps as “slow.”** `concread`, `concurrent-map`, and `flurry` were excluded for wrapper/API comparability and “too slow” comments, not because this report established a fair performance verdict. Investigate them separately if they are candidates.
4. **Re-run the noisy families before a production decision.** In particular, repeat 100K `String` growth, 3-thread `String` synchronization, and 1K clone/clone-then-write with more samples or repeated runs.
5. **Add the missing decision dimensions.** This suite does not measure memory footprint, tail latency, 4+ thread scaling, concurrent resize, adversarial collisions, or large values. Those can change the choice even when the medians here favor one map.
