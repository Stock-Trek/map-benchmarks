## The headline

**There is no single best map — the answer splits cleanly along the concurrency boundary, and along the hasher axis.**

- **For single-threaded use, `rustc-hash` is the default winner.** It is fastest in 26 of 37 configurations it participates in (all serial ones): lookups, misses, inserts, growth, remove, clear-and-reuse, clone-then-write, and the full mixed-workload throughput test at 1 thread. It typically leads ahash/hashbrown by 10–35%, and its lead is driven mostly by its hasher (FxHash), not its table structure.
- **For concurrent use (2+ threads), `txmap` is dominant.** It wins 38 of the 112 configurations — every contention, synchronization, and multi-threaded throughput benchmark except a handful — often by 1.3–2× over the nearest rival (dashmap, scc), with stable, low-variance measurements. Its transactional design (per-thread transaction cache) appears to be the reason. Its serial performance, however, is mediocre (1.3–3.9× behind rustc-hash on single-threaded throughput, worst for u64 keys), so it is a concurrency play, not an all-rounder.
- **Hashing cost, not map structure, separates the serial pack.** When a common hasher is forced, std, ahash and hashbrown are statistically tied (within ~3%) in nearly every read/write benchmark. std's poor out-of-the-box results are almost entirely its default SipHash; with a pluggable hasher it is fully competitive.

## Where specialist maps win

- **`indexmap`** wins every iteration benchmark by 2.8–3.4× (contiguous storage) and every String-keyed same-hasher growth/insert case (1.37× over hashbrown at 1M entries) — at the cost of somewhat slower random lookups.
- **`intmap`** is the fastest u64 lookup-hit/miss and remove out-of-the-box because it skips hashing entirely (integer keys used directly) — but it cannot take String keys.
- **`leapfrog`** is the only implementation that beats txmap — on u64 hot-spot contention (Zipfian) and single-key write mixes, where its fine-grained locking scales better than txmap's transactions (up to 2× faster). It requires `K: Copy`, so no String support.
- **`horde`** wins remove with a common hasher (1.5×), though the measurement is noisy.
- **`rpds-hash-trie-map`** (with immutable-chunkmap and imbl) clones in ~2 ns — an O(1) refcount bump via structural sharing — but pays for it on any copy-then-write workload, where persistent maps are the slowest.

## Stability and caveats

- The concurrency winner (txmap) and the serial winners are generally **low-variance** (median absolute deviation ≈ 0.8–1.4% of median). Noisy exceptions worth re-running before relying on their margins: `horde` remove (14% std dev), `leapfrog` remove/iterate (18–33%), `ahash` clone at 100K (27%), and the whole `concurrent-map` family (19–30% std dev — its wrapper clones the map per thread, a structural handicap).
- These are absolute numbers from a single run with no baseline. **Concurrency scaling was only tested at 2–3 threads.** Before adopting txmap for production, validate at higher thread counts and 1M+ entry sizes; its serial tax (up to 3.9× vs. rustc-hash on u64 single-thread workloads) matters in mixed deployments.

## Bottom line for engineers

Pick `rustc-hash` for single-threaded hot paths and `txmap` for shared, contended maps — then re-verify with your own thread counts and key shapes, because the gap between the two is entirely workload-dependent (txmap is 1.25× slower on String single-thread throughput but up to 3.9× on u64). If iteration order or String-keyed insertion dominates, `indexmap` is the sleeper winner; if your keys are integers, `intmap` is a free win; and if your workload is a u64 hot-spot, `leapfrog` out-contends even txmap.
