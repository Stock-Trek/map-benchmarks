# Executive Summary — Map Implementation Benchmarks

**Date:** 2026-08-22 · **Source:** Criterion.rs report, `target/criterion/` (112 benchmark groups, 1,403 measurements, median point estimates)

## Headline findings

**No single map wins everywhere — the field cleanly splits into three regimes: serial, concurrent, and persistent.** This is the one conclusion engineers should take away: picking a map is a workload decision, not a "best map" decision.

**Serial / out-of-the-box (default hashers): `rustc-hash` dominates.** It is the fastest implementation in 28 of the 37 benchmark groups it enters (75.7%), including all serial throughput, insert, growth, get-or-insert, and String lookup/remove runs. It wins `create` by well over an order of magnitude (0.33 ns per empty map vs 23 ns for ahash) and growth at every size (e.g. 31.7 ms to insert 1M u64 entries vs 33.1 ms for ahash and 56.9 ms for std). Its caveats are structural: FxHasher is not DoS-hardened, there is no way to supply a custom hasher (so it is absent from all `same-hasher` runs), and it is not concurrent. When the hasher is equalised (`same-hasher` runs), the field closes dramatically: `ahash`, `hashbrown`, and `std` are statistically tied (within 1–9% across insert, growth, lookups, get-or-insert, key sensitivity, clear-and-reuse). Practically, `hashbrown`/`ahash` are interchangeable for serial use, and `std` becomes competitive only with a fast hasher (SipHash costs it 2–8× out-of-the-box).

**Concurrency: the winner depends on key type and contention profile.** The u64 synchronization suite: `leapfrog` wins all 12 variants — it is barely affected by adding a third thread for reads (246 µs → 248 µs) and beats the next-best map by 1.3–2.3× (and the field by up to ~20×) on single-key u64 contention. But it only supports `Copy` keys/values (no String). For String keys, `txmap` is the strongest all-round concurrent map — it wins 7 of 12 contention variants, 9 of 12 two/three-thread throughput groups, and is within 10% in several others. It pays for this with a very expensive constructor (~887× slower than rustc-hash on `create`) and mediocre serial performance, so it should be constructed once and reused. Specialists matter at the extremes: `papaya` is the best read-only single-key map (both thread counts, String and close on u64), `dashmap` wins 2-thread String write-mixed synchronization, `scc` wins 3-thread String write-heavy synchronization and throughput, and under extreme Zipfian(2.0) skew the leaders shuffle (dashmap/papaya/scc at 3 threads). Per-operation throughput tells a mixed story: at 3 threads the concurrent winners are 2.4–5.8× slower per operation than the serial leader for u64 workloads, but for String workloads the best concurrent maps are actually *faster* per operation than the serial leader (0.76–0.93×), because the hashing/string work parallelises. Reach for concurrent maps when threads genuinely share a map — the win is workload-dependent.

**Persistent and specialised structures are workload-specific.** `rpds::HashTrieMap` clones in O(1) (1.3 ns regardless of map size vs ~157 µs for a deep-copy of a 100K map) — but is 3–40× slower at nearly everything else; `imbl` and `immutable-chunkmap` are similar. Choose these only for snapshot/copy-heavy designs with modest write rates. `indexmap` wins every iteration benchmark by 2.5–3× (contiguous entry array) and most same-hasher String growth runs; note its wrapper uses `swap_remove` (removal does not preserve insertion order). `intmap` is the fastest u64 lookup/remove path (1.47 ns/hit vs 1.9 ns for rustc-hash) but is integer-only. `btreemap`, `crossbeam-skiplist`, `starshard`, and `hashlink` never won any group; `concurrent-map` ranged from ~1.8× to 34.5× behind across concurrent workloads and its results should be treated with caution (see below).

## Data quality and cautions

Measurement quality is generally high — most groups have MAD/median under 2%, and **every outright fastest result is stable** (winners' MAD under ~3%), so the rankings are not noise. Three caveats:

1. **`txmap` is the noisiest fast implementation** — 5.5–7.2% MAD while placing 2nd in five 3-thread String synchronization groups; re-run with a longer window before quoting precise numbers for hot-key String workloads.
2. **`concurrent-map`'s results are unreliable.** Its wrapper is an unusual shim (the crate is `Send` but not `Sync`; the harness clones a master map into per-thread thread-locals each iteration), and two of its measurements show 11–23% MAD. Treat its relative performance as approximate pending validation of the harness.
3. **This is absolute data only** — there is no baseline/change dataset, so nothing here measures regressions over time. Additionally, `out-of-the-box` numbers conflate hasher quality with data-structure quality; only the `same-hasher` groups isolate the structure. All timings are per benchmark iteration (per-element costs are derived inline in the full report).

## Recommended defaults

- **Serial, default hasher:** `rustc-hash`, unless DoS-resistance of the hash matters, in which case `ahash` (essentially tied, ~1–9% behind).
- **Serial, custom hasher:** `ahash`/`hashbrown` (interchangeable; `std` equally fast with the same hasher).
- **Concurrent mixed workloads:** `txmap` (String), `leapfrog` (u64-only). For read-only hot keys: `papaya`. For write-heavy hot keys at 3 threads: `scc` (String) / `leapfrog` (u64).
- **Iteration-heavy:** `indexmap`. **Snapshot-heavy:** `rpds` (or `imbl`/`immutable-chunkmap`). **u64-only hot paths:** `intmap`.
